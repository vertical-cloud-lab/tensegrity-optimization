\section{Printability Screens and As-Built Resolution}
\label{si:printability}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The constant-mass projection of the main text (Section~3.1) scales every
candidate design before printing. This section records the two printability
screens applied to the projected designs and what the print record shows
about them, and the as-built scatter in mass.

\paragraph{Printability screens.} We also checked each projected design against two printability
limits. Both were recorded as warnings, and neither was used to reject
or change a design. The campaign code evaluates both on the as-printed
geometry of every recommended design
(\texttt{bo/\allowbreak t3\_prism\_bo\_campaign.py} on the campaign branch,
\href{\repo/pull/102}{PR~\#102}). It flags a design in the batch tables
as ``envelope ${>}$ 250~cm$^3$'' or ``cable ${<}$ 3.0~mm''. The first compares $\pi R^2 H$ with
\SI{250}{cm^3}, where $R$ is the radius of the circle through the
joint centers and $H$ is the height between joint centers. The
\SI{250}{cm^3} value is a placeholder size limit carried over from an
earlier simulation study. It is not the printer's build volume, and
because it is measured to the joint centers it undercounts the printed
part. Seven printed specimens (five designs) exceeded it. The second
limit is a \SI{3.0}{mm} minimum on the printed TPU cable diameter,
adopted after the thin, unsupported top cables of early PETG test
prints failed. Because the projection scales the whole cell, a
design at the \SI{3.0}{mm} lower bound of the cable variable can print
thinner. Nine specimens printed with cables of 2.51 to \SI{2.78}{mm},
including all four batch-4 specimens that reduced the peak shock
(main text, Section~4.2). With hand-painted tendon supports
(Section~\ref{si:supports}), all nine printed. One had
stringing, one had a slight tendon indent, and seven had no logged
defect. The most common tendon defect was a TPU strand that did not
fuse to the tendon and came away from it. It was logged on five
specimens, all with cables of 3.28 to \SI{4.23}{mm}. The \SI{3.0}{mm}
value is therefore a conservative rule for this workflow. Our prints
do not show that thinner cables cause more defects.

\paragraph{As-built resolution.} The nozzle diameter, the slicer line width,
and support interaction discretize the as-built cross-sections, so the
effective search granularity is coarser than the nominal continuous box.
The \SI{3.0}{mm}
lower bound on the TPU cable diameter is likewise a limit of this
workflow. It is the printability minimum described above, set by
printing unsupported cables and by the slicer's auto-support
detection on the H2D printer used in this study. Two sets of repeated prints bound the
fabrication scatter in mass. One seed design printed three times has a
mass standard deviation of \SI{0.457}{g}, the value used in the
observation noise model (main text, Section~3.4). The batch-3
plate, also printed three times, averaged 19.59, 19.86, and
\SI{20.07}{g} per print, so its second and third prints ran
\SI{0.27}{g} and \SI{0.48}{g} heavier specimen for specimen. That is a
per-print flow offset rather than random scatter.

\section{Joint-Design Study}
\label{si:joints}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
We developed the strut-to-cable junction through an OpenSCAD study of five
candidate joints (Designs~A to E: anchor-bulb, dovetail, TPU-sleeve overmold,
eyelet-loop, and TPU-rebar), followed by a sixth, captive-core design
(Design~F). Every campaign specimen uses Design~F, a captive TPU core inside
a PLA shell. At each strut end the tendons meet in a TPU core that is too
large to pass back out through the shell's tendon bores. The joint therefore
holds mechanically even though PLA and TPU bond weakly, and the shell keeps
the soft tendon away from layer-line failure at the transition. The campaign CAD
(\texttt{cad/t3-prism/t3-prism.scad} on the campaign branch) builds this
joint with \texttt{use\_captive\_core = true}.

Full results, renders, and the Phase-3/Phase-4 design reviews are documented in
\href{\repo/pull/39}{PR~\#39} (joint design) and
\href{\repo/pull/35}{PR~\#35} (CAD parameterization and print history).

\section{Support Strategy for Soft Members}
\label{si:supports}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Near-vertical TPU tendons are otherwise unsupported during printing. For the
printed campaign batches, we disabled automatic support generation and
painted PLA edge supports manually in Bambu Studio along each tendon. We
tightened the support-to-object XY clearance from \SI{0.35}{mm} to
\SI{0.2}{mm} during tuning on the S0 reference prism
(\href{\repo/issues/98}{issue~\#98}). Support removal is the leading source of
the cosmetic defects that we log per specimen in the print key
(Section~\ref{si:printkey}). They are surface stringing and, on two seed
specimens, a TPU strand that did not fuse to a bottom tendon and came away from
it.

In parallel, we built an automated alternative in software and archived
it, but no campaign specimen used it. Every campaign specimen was printed
with the manually painted supports described above. The alternative
combines a tensegrity-specific Bambu Studio recipe (support threshold angle
lowered from \SI{40}{\degree} to \SI{10}{\degree}, support material matched
to the rigid extruder) with narrowing support pillars placed by ray-casting
against the underside of the printable mesh. The narrowing-pillar
workflow is documented in
\href{\repo/pull/66}{PR~\#66}, and the H2D multi-part material assignment fix
in \href{\repo/pull/64}{PR~\#64}.

\section{Print Parameters and Process Control}
\label{si:process}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Table~\ref{tab:si-print-params} lists the multi-material print parameters,
transcribed from the archived as-printed Bambu Studio project. The joint
geometry is described in Section~\ref{si:joints} and the support strategy in
Section~\ref{si:supports}.

\paragraph{Moisture and nozzle management.} TPU is hygroscopic, so we
fed the soft filament from a heated dry box held near or below
\SI{8}{\percent} relative humidity, logged per print. Midway through the seed batch the
TPU extruder developed a progressive drop in flow (a partial clog,
compounded by a stray PLA fragment found inside the extruder). We
resolved it by performing a cold pull and moving the TPU to a dedicated
high-flow \SI{0.6}{mm} nozzle, after which print quality was restored.
We identify the affected and post-fix specimens in the print key so
that any nozzle-related covariate can be checked against the measured
objectives.

\section{Drop-Tower Protocol and Rig Characterization}
\label{si:drop}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The campaign fixture is a Lansmont Model~23 shock test system with a Test
Partner~4 data-acquisition console. This section summarizes the
development history behind the protocol in the main text.

\paragraph{Protocol.} The drop height is \SI{60}{in}, with the specimen
upright on the carriage plate. We discard the first two drops of each
session as seating warm-up. Session length differs by batch. Seed sessions
contribute 101 valid captures per specimen (103 for autv5r), the batch-2
record lists 21 to 22 valid captures per specimen, and batch-3 and batch-4
sessions recorded 20 drops per specimen. Exact per-session counts
live in the archived per-drop records, whose two generations differ
on whether the count is quoted before or after the warm-up discard.
Section 3.3 of the main text justifies the shortening. The
statistical minimum from the pre-campaign variance study is five
stabilized drops, and a truncation replay of the seed sessions
preserves the full-session transmissibility ranking at twenty drops.
The three prints of batch 3 also showed that the error budget is
dominated by seating and print variation rather than session length. We record
three high-speed video clips (\SI{959}{fps}) per specimen. From
batch 4 on, we film them at drops 1, 10, and 20 so that clips pair
deterministically with captures. The camera cannot resolve the
millisecond-scale impact pulse (one to two frames), so the
accelerometer channels produce the objectives and the video
documents the post-impact rebound and recovery. Synchronized
laser-Doppler vibrometry is reserved for follow-up characterization
of selected designs. Testing takes roughly \SI{42}{s} per
drop, so about
ninety minutes per seed specimen and fifteen per later-batch specimen.
Capture settings:
\SI{1.25}{MHz} sample rate, \SI{100}{ms} record, \SI{2}{ms} pre-trigger,
\SI{150}{G} trigger on the base channel, AC-coupled ICP conditioning.
After a settings-loss event, we re-verified the console settings
digit-for-digit against an archived screenshot and confirmed data
continuity to within about 1\%. Per the review instruction of 2026-09-02, the
per-session capture counts above should be re-verified by a person
against the committed per-drop records before submission.

\paragraph{Impact surface.} A blinded $3\times3$ crossover experiment over
three candidate polyurethane mat arrangements (single \SI{0.25}{in} Shore
70~OO, single \SI{0.5}{in} Shore 60~OO, stacked) selected the single
\SI{0.5}{in} Shore 60~OO sheet (\href{\repo/issues/88}{issue~\#88},
\href{\repo/pull/86}{PR~\#86}). Unlike the earlier felt stack, the mat does
not measurably compact over hundred-drop sessions. Sessions
recorded as ``5 felts'' in early protocol documents were physically four felt
sheets plus one cardboard layer.

\paragraph{Sensors and calibration.} Base input: single-axis piezoelectric
accelerometer on the carriage plate (\SI{1.059}{mV/G}, full scale
\SI{9443}{G}). Top response: Dytran 3133A4 triaxial accelerometer
(X/Y/Z 0.690/0.667/\SI{0.734}{mV/G}) seated in a printed pocket at the top
vertex, mounted per ISO~5348. We retired an earlier single-axis channel
after range clipping. We cross-calibrated the two base-capable channels in
place over fifteen co-located drops. The ratio was $0.953 \pm 0.001$ at
CFC-180 (per-drop $0.952 \pm 0.004$) and stable across drop heights
(\href{\repo/pull/74}{PR~\#74}).

\paragraph{Filtering correction.} We implemented the SAE~J211/1
channel-frequency-class filter used for all reported numbers from the
standard's published coefficients. An earlier in-house implementation passed the
two-pass (forward-backward) corner frequency to a single-pass design, which
narrows every channel class by roughly 20\% (an effective CFC-144 where
CFC-180 was intended). We identified the discrepancy during the analysis
audit of \href{\repo/issues/94}{issue~\#94} and corrected it before the
campaign numbers were produced.

\paragraph{Processing pipeline.} For each capture, we calibrate and
CFC-filter every channel independently. We form the top-vertex magnitude
$\lVert\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{top}}(t)\rVert$ from the three filtered
triaxial components and search for the two peaks entering $t_{180}$
within a short window (approximately \SI{1.5}{ms}) of first contact, so the
peaks need not be simultaneous. We estimate the baseline from the record
tail, because at healthy tower speed the mat-contact foot begins before the
\SI{2}{ms} pre-trigger window. $\Delta v$ is the integral of the baselined
base channel over the impact pulse and doubles as the rig-health check.
Drops are discarded per the warm-up rule. Table~\ref{tab:si-processing}
collects the operational analysis parameters in one place. Values known
from the protocol are stated numerically. The remaining thresholds
are to be transcribed verbatim from the version-tagged campaign analysis
code at its archival release, so that the table matches the code of
record.

\paragraph{Rig health and repair history.} We check rig health against
the impact velocity change $\Delta v$ recovered from the base channel.
Over an uninterrupted 100-drop session the $\Delta v$ coefficient of
variation (CV, the standard deviation divided by the mean) was 0.54\%
with a flat drift slope. Within a session, $\Delta v$ and the CFC-180 input
typically ease down by 1 to 4\% as the polyurethane mat warms, and
recover fully after a rest of minutes. After a mat rest or re-seat,
the first 5 to 10 drops carry the settling transient, which is why
the warm-up discard exists. The arrest velocity has visibly
stabilized as cumulative drop count grew, so we keep the
$\Delta v$ record. During commissioning, two guide-rod locking pins
failed, and we discovered and recommissioned a defeated pin-park
safety interlock. The
pin-related friction produced a measurable $\Delta v$ deficit that resolved
after repair (\href{\repo/issues/92}{issue~\#92}). We have performed
more than 4{,}400 instrumented drops across roughly 80 printed specimens
over about four months of commissioning, characterization,
and campaign sessions (tallied through
\href{\repo/issues/101}{issue~\#101} plus the batch records). The
campaign batches themselves contribute about 1{,}500 stabilized
captures including the second print of batch 3, and its third print
adds 162 more.

\paragraph{Seating sensitivity, the between-seat rule, and the seat
gauge.} The three prints of batch 3 (main text, Section 4.2) showed
that a session can be internally stable and still misrepresent its
design. The 1.251 session passed every within-session check, and the
same design's two later prints read 1.029 and 1.045. Two standing
conventions followed. First, under the \emph{between-seat replication
rule}, any single-seating value that would drive a decision requires
confirmation on an independent re-seat. Second, we defined an advisory
\emph{seat gauge}, the broadband ratio of CFC-1000 to CFC-180
transmissibility. It reads 1.00 to 1.07 on most sessions, and we
treat a session reading 1.15 or more as a re-seat candidate. The
third print is the first replicate data collected after the gauge was
defined (\mbox{2026-09-12}). Its one gross outlier (dran35, 10.1\% above its design's
median over the three prints) read 2.43, and its other eight sessions
read 1.01 to 1.06. Over all 27 batch-3 sessions, the four reading 1.15
or more were each their design's highest value, and the gauge
correlates with the deviation from the design median (Spearman
$\rho = 0.52$, $p = 0.006$). Both conventions, with the
session-by-session drift-watch record, live in the drop-test datasets
on \href{\repo/pull/86}{PR~\#86}. The three-print comparison is
archived with the manuscript data
(\texttt{t3-prism-bo-round3-three-print.csv}, generated by
\texttt{scripts/\allowbreak figures/\allowbreak round3\_three\_print\_analysis.py}).

\paragraph{Objective provenance and pre-registered review.} The objectives
evolved through a measurability review
(\href{\repo/pull/97}{PR~\#97}). Per-specimen crush energy in joules is not
recoverable from this rig, because it has no falling-mass or displacement
channel and the specimens stay elastic. The campaign responses are therefore the
filtered peak-acceleration ratio and the mass-weighted rebound, built
on a provisional rebound fraction, with quasi-static
crush metrics deferred to a load-frame campaign. We pre-registered an
adversarial review of the objective choices, run with the Edison
Scientific analysis agent (task \texttt{3e398131}, archived in the
repository). It
recommended accounting for specimen-level rather than per-drop noise, treating
the rebound fraction as a candidate constraint pending a
restrained-versus-unrestrained validation with synchronized video,
and reserving print capacity for replicate specimens. The main text
records how the campaign responded to these recommendations. We
printed and tested the batch-3 plate three times, which measured
the print-to-print scatter directly and produced the between-seat
replication rule. The rebound fraction remains a
candidate constraint, and the
surrogate continues to ingest per-session standard errors, with the
replication evidence carried as an interpretation bound rather than
as an inflated noise term. We plan to act on the reserved-replicate
recommendation through the replicate rank-stability study of the
main-text Methods. That study will re-print the best-, mid-, and
worst-predicted in-sample designs in nominal triplicate and test whether the
predicted ordering survives print-to-print and seating variation.

\section{Planned Quasi-Static Extension}
\label{si:quasistatic}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The drop test loads the
specimens in their elastic regime. The crush lasts 1 to \SI{3}{ms} and
the specimen recovers, so crush-energy metrics such as specific energy
absorption (SEA) and compaction efficiency are not recoverable from
these captures by any reprocessing. We plan a quasi-static compression
campaign on a benchtop load frame, with a low-range load cell
appropriate to the expected cell stiffness,
to supply the complementary crush metrics
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SEA} \;=\; \frac{1}{m}\!\int_{0}^{\delta_d}\! F(\delta)\,
  \mathrm{d}\delta,
  \qquad
  \eta_c \;=\; \frac{\int_{0}^{\delta_d}\! F(\delta)\,\mathrm{d}\delta}{F_{\max}\,\delta_d},
\end{equation}
with the densification displacement $\delta_d$ taken at the maximum of
the energy-absorption efficiency curve and $F_{\max}$ the peak force
within $[0,\delta_d]$. We follow the standard cellular-solid
treatments~\citep{gibson1997cellular, avalle2001characterization,
tan2005dynamic, michailidis2011establishment} and mirror the
quasi-static protocol of Pajunen et~al.~\citep{pajunen2019design}.
These metrics will be reported as a complementary characterization of
selected designs rather than as objectives of the drop-tower campaign.
Slip resistance and traction at the specimen-to-floor interface,
governed by the tip geometry rather than the internal architecture,
follow ISO 11334-4~\citep{iso11334-4} for walking-aid tips and are out
of scope for the present axial-impact study.

\section{Printed-Mass Model}
\label{si:mass}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
We projected the seed batch onto constant \emph{solid} CAD mass
($m^{\star} = \SI{30.95}{g}$), yet the specimens weigh
\SIrange{18.50}{22.29}{g}. The gap arises because PLA struts print at
partial infill (15\% grid, two walls) while thin TPU cables print near
solid, and the PLA-to-TPU volume split swings widely across the design
box. A two-stage calibrated model closes this gap
(\href{\repo/pull/102}{PR~\#102},
\texttt{bo/t3\_prism\_mass\_model.py}). Analytic member volumes are corrected
to rendered-solid grams (one factor per material). Rendered-solid grams
are then mapped to weighed grams through a wall-plus-infill term in the as-printed
strut diameter (fitted infill 23.6\%, wall \SI{1.18}{mm}, TPU factor 0.744).
The model's residual standard deviation is \SI{0.378}{g}. For context, the
three available prints of design 08 have a sample standard deviation of
\SI{0.457}{g}. Three prints of one geometry are insufficient to establish a
general process repeatability limit. Run in reverse inside the
projection, the model holds \emph{printed} grams constant without
running the slicer. For a target mass, the projection finds the
uniform scale factor at which the predicted printed mass equals the
target, and we print the design at that scale. Bisection is enough
for this, because predicted mass rises monotonically with scale.
Batches 3 and 4 used a target of \SI{20.23}{g}, the mass of the
printed reference specimen. Batch mass CV
fell from 5.5\% (tested seed specimens) and 8.7\% (batch 2, still
solid-mass-projected) to 1.7\% (batch 3) and 2.3\% (batch 4). The
weighings sit at or below the model's estimate as a per-print flow
offset ($-0.52$, $-0.25$, and \SI{-0.04}{g} for the three prints of
batch 3 and \SI{-0.26}{g} for batch 4). In a first test of the
model's infill terms, regressing the print-averaged mass
residuals of the eighteen batch-3 specimens on the two commanded
infills gives slopes indistinguishable from zero over the realized
13 to 34\% spread. We will recalibrate the session term with the
accumulated weighings before the next batch's projection. Two batch-2
recommendation sets exist in the record.
The batch actually sliced and sent to the printer was generated
before this correction, under the seed batch's solid-mass
projection. A subsequently regenerated set applies the
constant-printed-mass projection and seeded the batch-3 tooling.
Both are archived with the manuscript data
(\texttt{round2-as-printed-solid-mass-projection.csv},
\texttt{round3-candidate-constant-printed-mass.csv}).

\section{Metal-Analog Validation Plan}
\label{si:metal-validation}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
A planned validation campaign will rebuild a subset of the $T_3$-prism
designs as metal analogs, using the same $R$, $H$, $\theta$, and
member-diameter parameterization as the printed PLA/TPU specimens. The
analogs will use hollow aluminum rods for the compression members and
stainless-steel threaded cables for the tension members. To probe transfer
across material systems rather than only confirming the optimum, the
selected designs will deliberately span the predicted performance range.
Representatives the surrogate predicts to be worst-, mid-, and
best-performing will each be built in both material systems and
characterized under the identical drop protocol of
Section~\ref{si:drop}. We treat this as a planned, exploratory comparison
rather than a preregistered validation. The deformation modes, joint
compliance, friction, pretension behavior, and strain-rate sensitivity all
differ between the PLA/TPU and aluminum/steel systems, so agreement is not
expected a priori. The protocol details that would let the comparison
distinguish geometric rank transfer from material-system and mass confounds
remain to be fixed and will be tabulated before any metal specimen is built.
They are the aluminum alloy and temper, tube wall thickness, cable
construction, joint hardware and torque, specimen and payload masses,
replicate count, and tie-handling rules. The intended rank statistic is the
Spearman rank-correlation coefficient $\rho_s$ between the two material
systems' orderings of the primary campaign metric ($t_{180}$ under the
identical drop protocol), reported with an exact permutation test.
Table~\ref{tab:si-metal-validation} and Fig.~\ref{fig:si-metal-validation}
will report the measured PLA/TPU ranking and the
hollow-aluminum/stainless-steel ranking on $t_{180}$, with the velocity
loss index and, later, quasi-static specific energy absorption (SEA,
Section~\ref{si:quasistatic}) as secondary rankings. Agreement between the
two primary rankings would be preliminary evidence consistent with transfer
beyond the as-printed polymers. Disagreement would not isolate which
material or joint difference caused the change.
