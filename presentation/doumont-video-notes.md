## Overview

A presentation is all about the right content, displayed the right way, at the
right time.

- Verbal communication is sequential.
- Message is *interpretation* of the information.
  - Ensure the audience can get the most out of what you're doing.
  - Don't just give them information — help them understand it, interpret it,
    and internalize it.

## Doumont's suggested rules for a presentation

0. Ensure you have **messages** for your audience, not just words.
1. **Adapt to your audience.**
   - Think like the audience, not the speaker.
2. **Maximize signal-to-noise ratio.**
   - Reduce noise (animations, laser pointers, anything that pulls attention
     away from your message).
3. **Use effective redundancy.**
   - Tell your message in word *and* on slide: someone deaf should understand
     your messages just as well as someone blind.
   - Ineffective redundancy is clutter appearing on every slide (e.g., the
     date).

## When creating slides

- If the audience doesn't need it, why put it on the slides?
- People shouldn't have to count the items on a slide to know how many are
  there.
- Don't use your slides as your "presentation notes."
  - Don't show your notes to the audience.
  - **A bad slide is worse than no slide at all.**
- **One message per slide** is a good rule of thumb.
  - Prioritize the *so what* (or *therefore what*) of your message.
- Make it easy to interpret the information on the slide:
  - 3–5 items at most.
  - Align the title left.
  - Do not clutter slides with repetitive or unnecessary information: logos,
    affiliation, date, slide numbers.
- Doumont's suggested workflow:
  1. **Start with the title.**
     - Use a full sentence in the title.
     - The title is the main message of the slide.
     - Make it easy to read: left-aligned, with intentional line breaks.
  2. **Do the visual next.** Optimize for viewing / taking in the message.
- Try printing the slideshow as a handout, 6 per page, to test visibility.

## General preparation

- Planning for audience, content, and structure matters more than materials
  (i.e., slides).
- Try using one sheet of paper for presentation preparation.
- It's most important that your presentation is:
  - well planned, structured, and delivered;
  - **if you don't have a lot of time to prepare, don't focus on making
    slides.**
- Practice the presentation a few times. Practice at least once *without* your
  slideshow!
- If doing a handout, keep it separate from the slides you're preparing.
- Impress the audience with your body.
  - Use gestures, not laser pointers.
  - "Control" the audience with your body — keep their attention on your
    presentation.
