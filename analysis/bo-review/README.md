# BO methods review: noise model, reference point, filament settings, batch terms, lengthscales, MCMC

**The ask ([PR #121, 2026-11-10](https://github.com/vertical-cloud-lab/tensegrity-optimization/pull/121)):**
sgbaird forwarded Juan Florez-Coronel's review of the BO methods with a
response to each point. This directory answers them in the review's order.
All tables are regenerated by `summarize.py` into [`results.md`](results.md);
the numbers quoted below come from there.

Data and folds are those of [`../logocv-baselines`](../logocv-baselines/README.md):
44 articles from five print sessions, 35 leave-one-design-out (LOGO) folds
with reprint twins held out together, the archived per-article SEMs, and
the campaign's input bounds. The campaign code is
`bo/t3_prism_bo_campaign.py` on `claude/issue-98-20260821-0103` (`bbf7a62`
for the LOGO protocol) and `claude/me2-objective-bo-20261029` (`f66c363`,
the current batch-5 generator).

## 1. Fixed noise vs learned noise vs replicate-based SEMs

> Could you compare fixed noise against inferred noise and against
> replicate-based SEMs using grouped cross-validation?

`logocv_noise_models.py` fits the same surrogate three ways on the same 35
LOGO folds:

- **fixed**: `train_Yvar = SEM^2`, what the campaign gave Ax;
- **learned**: no `train_Yvar`, one noise level per objective learned with the
  other hyperparameters;
- **replicate**: `train_Yvar = SEM^2 + s_print^2`, with `s_print` the pooled
  within-design sd of the reprint twins *in the training fold* (8 or 9 pairs;
  the held-out pair never sets its own noise). Each article is one print, so
  the noise of its mean is the drop-level SEM plus the print-to-print
  scatter the twins measure. `s_print` is 0.057 for t180 (0.023 when the
  drran7/2dran7 pair is held out: one tripped-seat session carries most of
  it) and 4.2 mJ for E_reb, against median SEMs of 0.00055 and 0.20 mJ.

Surrogates: BoTorch's `SaasFullyBayesianSingleTaskGP` (the class Ax's
SAASBO builds) with NUTS at the campaign budget (512 warmup, 256 samples,
thinning 16), and BoTorch's default `SingleTaskGP` (MAP). Both in BoTorch
0.18.1, whose NUTS runs in NumPyro, about five times faster than Ax 0.5.0's
Pyro sampler. The fixed-noise SAAS run reproduces the campaign's Ax SAASBO
result on the same folds (t180 rho_s +0.30 against Ax's +0.32, E_reb -0.51
against -0.40), so the faster stack is a fair stand-in.

Twelve campaign parameters, held out (rho_s = Spearman, two-sided permutation p):

| surrogate | noise | t180 rho_s | t180 R2_oos | E_reb rho_s | E_reb R2_oos |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| SAAS (NUTS 512/256) | fixed SEMs (campaign) | +0.30 (0.050) | +0.15 | -0.51 (<0.001) | -0.05 |
| SAAS | learned (BoFire SAAS run of this PR) | +0.52 | +0.37 | +0.48 | +0.25 |
| SAAS | replicate-based SEMs | +0.52 (0.001) | +0.37 | +0.38 (0.012) | +0.12 |
| MAP GP | fixed SEMs | +0.45 (0.002) | +0.13 | -0.09 (0.57) | -0.03 |
| MAP GP | learned | +0.48 (0.001) | +0.29 | +0.53 (<0.001) | +0.34 |
| MAP GP | replicate-based SEMs | +0.45 (0.003) | +0.31 | +0.51 (<0.001) | +0.26 |

In the six-parameter spaces the pattern is the same and starker (full
tables in `results.md`): with the SEMs as fixed noise the MAP GP ranks
held-out t180 at +0.04 (six) and -0.01 (six + sim), and with either
replacement at +0.41 to +0.53.

**Reading.** Replicate-based SEMs fix what fixed SEMs break, and do about as
well as learning the noise: identical on t180, a little behind on E_reb for
SAAS (+0.38 against +0.48). The two remedies agree because they estimate the
same quantity: the learned noise sd (0.034 to 0.050 for t180, 2.4 to 3.1 mJ
for E_reb across the runs of this PR) sits at 60 to 90 percent of the
replicate `s_print`. What breaks the fixed-noise model is described in
`../logocv-baselines/README.md` (follow-up 1): told that the twins are
known to within their SEMs, the GP explains their 26-SEM (t180) and 6-SEM
(E_reb) differences with the only inputs that differ between twins, the
weighed mass and (in the twelve space) the print settings, and pins the mass
lengthscale at its floor. Juan is right that line 857 of the campaign script
("repeated designs are exactly how Ax infers the noise floor the per-drop
SEM understates") is wrong: with SEMs supplied, Ax infers no noise floor.

**Recommendation.** Learn the noise, or give each article
`SEM^2 + s_print^2` from the reprints. Learning it needs no bookkeeping and
did at least as well here. A heavier-tailed likelihood (or screening the
tripped-seat sessions) is the next refinement: one pair carries most of the
t180 replicate variance. Neither was tried here.

## 2. Objective thresholds

> No objective thresholds or acquisition classes are set, Ax therefore
> infers a reference point that has been tight in every snapshot [...]
> Setting threshold= on both objectives and pinning botorch_acqf_class
> (e.g. qLogNEHVI) would make the acquisition and the reports measure the
> same thing.

**Confirmed from the archived snapshots** (`inferred_thresholds.py`, which
reads `gen_metadata["objective_thresholds"]` of every generated trial, no
refit; `data/inferred-thresholds.json`):

| snapshot (generated batch) | inferred t180 bound | inferred rebound bound |
|---|--:|--:|
| run 1 (batch 2) | 1.011 | 6.20 mJ |
| run 2 (batch 3, first version) | 1.011 | 6.20 mJ |
| run 3 (batch 3) | 1.011 | 6.19 mJ |
| run 4 (batch 4) | 1.011 | 6.19 mJ |
| run 5 (batch 5, `me`) | 1.065 | 6.31 mJ |
| run 6 (batch 5, `me2`) | 1.065 | 8.75 mg |

The reported hypervolume uses (1.35, 15 mJ), or (1.35, 50 mg) under `me2`.
Corny7 (0.803, 9.37 mJ; 20.0 mg) lies outside every inferred box, so the
acquisition gave the campaign's best attenuator's corner no hypervolume
credit at all.

**The acquisition class.** Ax 0.5.0 already picks
`qLogNoisyExpectedHypervolumeImprovement` for two objectives
(`choose_botorch_acqf_class`), so pinning it records the choice and guards
against a version change; it does not change batch 5.

**Batch 5 regenerated** (`regenerate_batch5.py`): the campaign script at
`f66c363`, run exactly as for run 6 (`--round 6 --batch-number 5`, `me2`,
seed 42, 44 articles + 2 pending, 20.23 g), with only the acquisition setup
changed. Every variant refits the same model with the same seed, so the
differences come from the acquisition. See the table in `results.md`
(section 2 and 3) and the reading below.

## 3. Filament settings replaced after the acquisition

> Ax optimizes all 12 parameters per article, but a plate can only print at
> one filament setting. The script therefore replaces Ax's filament values
> with a single Sobol point afterward [...] Could you fix the filament point
> first (e.g. via process_bounds in gen_search_space) and let Ax optimize
> the remaining parameters around it?

**Where this was decided.** PR #102,
[comment 5484447933](https://github.com/vertical-cloud-lab/tensegrity-optimization/pull/102#issuecomment-5484447933)
(2026-08-31): "instead of printing separate plates with different filament
parameters, simply choose the one best predicted specimen out of the batch
and determine what the optimal predicted print parameters [...] would be,
and then we'll just let those same print parameters apply everywhere. Since
you won't have any data with variation to learn from, start out by just
Sobol sampling that initial design". `fixed_features` came up earlier, on
PR #33 ([comment 5377452641](https://github.com/vertical-cloud-lab/tensegrity-optimization/pull/33#issuecomment-5377452641),
2026-08-22), but for mass, not for the filament settings.

**What the code does** (`t3_prism_bo_campaign.py`, `main()`, unchanged from
commit `58ebb80` through `f66c363`): `ax_client.get_next_trials(9)` with all
twelve coordinates free, then every article's four filament coordinates are
overwritten by point `batch - 3` of a seeded Sobol sequence, and the
proposed trials are abandoned and the overwritten ones attached. So neither
version of the instruction was implemented: the Sobol point was meant for
the first batch only, and it was always applied after the acquisition, not
before it. `gen_search_space(process_bounds=...)` exists but is never
called with narrowed filament bounds. The script prints the prediction
shift the substitution causes: 86 percent of a posterior t180 sd for the
superseded batch 46 to 54, 31 percent for run 5.

**The fix.** `AxClient.get_next_trials(fixed_features=FixedFeatures(...))`
in Ax 0.5.0 holds the four filament coordinates at the batch's point during
the acquisition optimization. `regenerate_batch5.py --fixed-filament`
passes the same Sobol point the script would have substituted (PLA 223 C /
24.7 mm3/s, TPU 240 C / 3.0 mm3/s), so the acquisition places the other
eight coordinates around it and the post-hoc substitution becomes a no-op
(prediction shift 0.12 percent of a posterior sd, against the substitution
it replaces). Results in the batch-5 table.

Choosing the filament point itself is a separate question. With one point
per batch, the honest options are the ones the campaign already discussed:
keep the Sobol sequence (exploration, but then fix it before the
acquisition), or pick the point the model prefers by optimizing the batch
acquisition value over candidate filament points in an outer loop. The
second needs a model that can tell filament effects from batch effects,
which item 4 says this one cannot.

## 4. Filament settings vs batch

> Filament settings change only between batches, so the GP sees any batch
> difference (spool, humidity, test sessions) as a filament effect. Would
> you drop those four parameters from the model, or add a batch term [...]

`batch_term.py` tests both on the MAP GP with learned noise (the
noise model of item 1; MAP so that 18 model-by-scheme runs fit in minutes):

- **drop**: `eight` = the six original parameters plus the two per-article
  infill densities, which do vary within a batch;
- **random intercept**: an additive per-session offset (a linear kernel on
  one-hot session columns, k = v [same session]). A new batch gets the
  design prediction with v added to its variance;
- **session order as an input** (sgbaird's "batch number as part of the
  search space"): print order 1 to 5 as one more ARD input. A new batch is
  an extrapolation along it.

Scored two ways: LOGO (every held-out article's session is in training) and
leave-one-session-out (LOBO, the situation of a new batch).

BATCH_TERM_READING

**Humidity, drying, spools.** The covariate table of the print audit
(`manuscript/data/t3-prism-article-print-covariates.csv`,
`scripts/figures/print_covariate_audit.py`) records chamber RH, days since
the TPU was dried and TPU roll age once per print session, not per article:
RH is 11 percent for r2d2c, drran and 2dran, 7 for corny and 8 for the
late seed articles, and the two earliest seed articles have none. So these
covariates are session labels too: five sessions cannot separate RH, drying,
spool, nozzle, test week and filament settings from one another or from a
session offset. No spool swap is documented after 2026-07-13, so spool age
is a calendar count. The record is complete enough to say that the
session-level covariates do not vary within a session, and not enough for
anything finer.

**Recommendation.** Until an experiment varies the filament settings within
a batch, drop the four filament settings from the model (or keep them out of
the acquisition with `fixed_features`). A session term helps only if the
next batch shares a session with data, which a new batch does not. The
experiment Juan suggests, the same designs at several filament settings on
one plate, is the split-plot design of commit `265bfe5` that was replaced on
2026-08-31; the replicate-study plates (same designs, three plates, one
setting) measure the session term without varying the settings.

## 5. SAASBO vs a standard ARD GP

> The cross-validation has only been run on SAASBO.

Yes, this PR ran it (`../logocv-baselines/README.md`): a scikit-learn ARD
Matern GP (ML-II), Ax 0.5.0's default `SingleTaskGP` (ARD RBF, MAP) and
BoFire's default GP, on the same 35 folds in three spaces. With the noise
learned, the fully Bayesian and MAP GPs are within what 44 articles can
resolve (rho_s differences under 0.2):

| | t180 rho_s (six / six + sim / twelve) | E_reb rho_s (six / six + sim / twelve) |
|---|---|---|
| SAASBO, Ax, learned noise | +0.53 / +0.55 / +0.55 | +0.27 / +0.33 / +0.51 |
| SAAS, BoFire, learned | +0.54 / +0.59 / +0.52 | +0.28 / +0.36 / +0.48 |
| Ax default GP (MAP), learned | +0.52 / +0.41 / +0.53 | +0.19 / +0.33 / +0.54 |
| BoFire default GP (MAP) | +0.53 / +0.41 / +0.48 | +0.19 / +0.28 / +0.53 |
| ARD Matern GP (ML-II, sklearn) | +0.51 / +0.41 / +0.55 | +0.24 / +0.21 / +0.41 |

SAAS is a little ahead on t180 R2_oos (+0.37 to +0.45 against +0.29 to
+0.40) and behind on E_reb in the twelve space (+0.23 to +0.25 against
+0.34). What the fully Bayesian fit buys here is calibration: its latent sd
covers 91 to 93 percent of held-out t180 against 73 to 89 percent for the
MAP GPs' latent sd. It costs 20 s (NumPyro) to 150 s (Pyro) per fit against
about a second. With fixed SEMs both fail, so the model class was never the
problem.

## 6 and 7. Importances, raw lengthscales, and the twelve-parameter model

> The t180 feature importances are close to uniform, which may mean the
> model is close to flat. The raw lengthscales would tell. [...] No
> importances exist yet for the 12-parameter model.

sgbaird's reading is right: Ax 0.5.0's `feature_importances` is
`1 / median lengthscale`, normalized to sum to 1
(`ax/models/torch/botorch.py::get_feature_importances_from_botorch_model`),
and the campaign's `t3_prism_bo_diagnostics.py` plots exactly that with an
IQR band over the draws. The normalization removes the overall scale, so it
cannot tell a flat model from a busy one. `importances_stability.py` reports
the raw median lengthscales on the unit cube, the learned noise and a direct
flatness measure: the sd of the posterior mean over 1024 Sobol points of the
fit space, next to the sd of the data. It does so for the round-2 model
(17 articles, six parameters: the archived importances) and for the
twelve-parameter model on all 44 articles, with fixed and learned noise.

## 8. MCMC stability across seeds and sample counts

> MCMC settings are left at defaults and differ between runs, so a
> stability check across seeds and sample counts would help.

Each fit above is repeated over four NUTS seeds at three budgets (256/128,
512/256 = the campaign's, 1024/512 warmup/samples, thinning 16). Across
seeds, `summary.json` reports the spread of each importance, which input
ranks first, the spread of the posterior mean at the training articles, and
a split-R-hat over the four seeds (one chain each) for every log
lengthscale.

MCMC_READING

Two records from this PR say the same at the level of LOGO-CV and of the
batch: the campaign's fixed-noise SAASBO moved its held-out t180 rho_s from
+0.08 to +0.32 between 64/128 and 512/256 NUTS, while learned-noise SAAS
moved by at most 0.04 between BoFire's 128/256 and 512/256
(`../logocv-baselines`, follow-up 2); and the run-6 control on the me2
branch, the same command rerun on a different software stack, changed the
batch-5 family split from 3/6 to 4/4/1.

## Files

- `logocv_noise_models.py`: LOGO-CV under fixed / learned / replicate noise,
  SAAS or MAP; outputs `data/logocv/<model>-<noise>-<space>/` (per-fold
  records, campaign-layout `logocv.csv`, `fits.json` with lengthscales and
  the fold's `s_print`, `state.json` with the scores).
- `regenerate_batch5.py`: the campaign script with `--thresholds` and/or
  `--fixed-filament`; `compare_batch5.py`: the comparison table.
  Outputs `data/batch5/` (suggestions and run records per variant).
- `inferred_thresholds.py`: the reference points Ax inferred, read from the
  archived snapshots; `data/inferred-thresholds.json`.
- `batch_term.py`: session terms, LOGO and leave-one-session-out;
  `data/batch-term/batch-term.json`.
- `importances_stability.py`: raw lengthscales, importances, flatness, seed
  and budget sweep; `data/importances/`.
- `summarize.py`: writes `results.md`.
