### 1. Mock Reviewer #1 — Design / Mechanical Engineering
**Recommendation: Major Revision**

**Major Concerns:**
1. **Bayesian Optimization Formulation and Implementation:** The current manuscript vaguely references a Gaussian-process surrogate but provides no mathematical details on the kernel choice (Matérn, RBF, etc.), handling of the categorical connectivity variables, or the acquisition function formulation. The authors claim to use BoTorch with LogEI and qNEHVI but do not specify how constraints (e.g., peak transmitted force limits) are applied during the multi-objective search, nor how batch sizes ($q$) and iteration budgets ($T$) scale with the design space. 
2. **Missing Multifidelity Link:** The introduction claims a gap relative to "multifidelity BO," citing Mo et al. (2023), but then strictly focuses on "direct experimental data" without a low-fidelity simulation step. The paper does not appear to be multifidelity. The authors must clarify if a secondary fidelity level is being used (e.g., quasi-static vs. impact tests) or reframe the novelty claims. 
3. **Design Parameterization:** The categorical encoding of connectivity topology and the scaling of unit-cell tiling ($N_x \times N_y \times N_z$) drastically expands the search space size. The bounds, manufacturing feasibility constraints, and total dimensionality are currently omitted. It is not clear whether the experimental budget is sufficient to explore this space efficiently. 

**Minor Concerns / Editorial:**
1. The third "Contributions" bullet is completely blank in the PDF, likely due to a suppressed macro.
2. The term "tensegrity-inspired" is correctly used rather than "tensegrity," as FDM TPU is not an idealized inextensible cable. However, please formally define the criteria separating "tensegrity" from "tensegrity-inspired" in this work's unit cells.
3. The bibliography renders with repeated "(author?)" markers. Please fix your `.bst` or BibTeX data formatting. 
4. **Venue Fit:** The focus on design-space exploration, parameterization, and Bayesian optimization aligns strongly with the ASME JMD scope for Design Automation and Design of Experiments. This is an appropriate venue for the manuscript.

### 2. Mock Reviewer #2 — Biomechanics / Rehabilitation Engineering
**Recommendation: Major Revision**

**Major Concerns:**
1. **Clinical Loading Disconnect:** The authors motivate the work in Section 5 via long-term crutch use and upper-extremity overuse injuries. However, the experimental protocol uses a generic "drop-weight impact." Crutch tip loading in-vivo is highly complex: it involves an off-axis strike angle, rolling contact, a double-peak ground reaction force profile, and both axial and shear components. An axial drop-tower test does not reflect the slip/shear failure modes or biomechanical loading conditions of crutch use. 
2. **Translation Feasibility:** The manuscript suggests miniaturization to a 19–25 mm envelope. Scaling an architected multimaterial lattice down to 19 mm while maintaining internal FDM strut integrity, resolving the TPU skin, and sustaining physiological loads (hundreds of Newtons per crutch) poses immense additive manufacturing challenges not accounted for in the current macro-scale test parameters. 
3. **Repeated vs. Single Impact:** Crutch tips must endure thousands of cyclic loads per day. The experimental section only evaluates quasi-static compression and single drop-weight impacts. Although TPU "rate-dependent damping" is discussed, cyclic fatigue, heat buildup, and hysteresis behavior are completely absent from the protocol. 

**Minor Concerns / Editorial:**
1. No participant testing or IRB protocol is mentioned. If the end-goal is biomechanical, eventually user-in-the-loop kinetic/kinematic evaluation (e.g., motion capture + force plates) will be needed, though benchmarking a passive tip via instrumented loading is acceptable for an early-stage mechanical design paper.
2. The manuscript does not clearly map "specific energy absorption (SEA)" and "compaction efficiency" to the most clinically relevant metric: the peak transmitted force transferred to the user's wrist/shoulder.

### 3. Mock Reviewer #3 — Additive Manufacturing / Materials
**Recommendation: Major Revision**

**Major Concerns:**
1. **Critical Material Discrepancies:** There is a severe mismatch between the framing and the text. The project scope aims at a TPU+PETG matrix fabricated on an IDEX printer (e.g., Bambu, Raise3D, etc.), but the manuscript body explicitly states the use of **PLA+TPU** continuously across 51 instances in the text and the introduction. This must be corrected.
2. **Interface Adhesion and Core-Wrapping Validity:** The core-wrapping strategy (Ye et al., 2023) was developed to resolve poor interfacial adhesion between soft and rigid FDM polymers. However, TPU and PETG (the actual intended materials) have substantially better innate layer adhesion than TPU and PLA. The authors must justify whether core-wrapping is strictly mechanically necessary for TPU/PETG, or if it introduces unnecessary geometric constraints and slicing complexities. 
3. **Process Controls:** There is no detail provided regarding the FFF slicing parameters. Variables such as printing temperature, cooling, layer height, and retraction heavily influence the mechanical performance and porosity of thin FFF struts. 
4. **Alternative Architectures:** The authors should briefly discuss why FDM/FFF was chosen over more isotropic elastomeric lattice technologies like SLA/DLP (using flexible resins) or SLS/MJF (using TPU powders), which do not suffer from the same Z-axis weakness or multimaterial interface failures. 

**Minor Concerns / Editorial:**
1. Fatigue data for rigid FFF struts is cited (Ezeh, Vanaei), but FFF fatigue is heavily dependent on the raster angle and infill. Ensure conservative design bounds explicitly account for inter-layer shear failure.

### Reviewers' Bibliographic Gaps

Given the current `references.bib`, the authors are missing key citations in several areas:

1. **Off-axis/crutch-tip biomechanical testing:** The authors cite clinical upper-extremity kinetic data (Requejo, Manocha, etc.) and general pneumatic/spring crutches (Xiao, Segura), but miss engineering test protocols for crutch tips. They should cite standard slip/friction testing for crutch tips (e.g., *ISO 11334-4* standard for walking aids, or *Basford et al. 1990* on crutch tip friction).
2. **TPU/PETG Adhesion:** The authors cite TPU/PLA adhesion/composites (Arifvianto, Ruwais), but lack literature on TPU/PETG interface strength in FDM, which is chemically distinct and generally superior (e.g., *Caminero et al. 2019* or similar work on multimaterial FFF adhesion). 
3. **Bayesian Optimization Constraints/Categoricals:** While the authors cite BoTorch, LogEI, and qNEHVI, they miss foundational literature on handling categorical variables (e.g., topologies) in BO (e.g., *Ru et al. 2020*, "BOCS", or *Garrido-Merchán & Hernández-Lobato 2020*). 
4. **Lattice Scaling/Miniaturization:** Literature on the limits of FFF resolution for architected elastomeric lattices. Scaling down to 19mm via FDM will hit nozzle diameter limits (typically 0.4mm), a known constraint mapped by studies on FFF lattice manufacturability.
