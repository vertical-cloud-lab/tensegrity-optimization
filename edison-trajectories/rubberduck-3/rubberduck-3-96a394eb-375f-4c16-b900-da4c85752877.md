## Iteration 3 of 5: framing, novelty, and scope

### Probe 1: Is the central novelty claim defensible against the cited literature?

**Hostile referee:** You write that “to our knowledge no prior study closes a BO loop over co-printed multi-material tensegrity-inspired hardware using physical impact measurements as the sole ground truth” (manuscript-body.tex, lines 241–247). Have you chosen qualifiers that actually distinguish this work, or assembled enough qualifiers to manufacture novelty?

**Author, from the attached record:** Each qualifier does real work.

- Pajunen 2019 reports a 3D-printable tensegrity-inspired structure and drop-impact experiments, but the manuscript describes it as a “single fabrication condition with no optimization loop” (lines 242–244; DOI `10.1016/j.matdes.2019.107966`).
- Pajunen 2021 concerns prestrain-induced bandgap tuning, not a physical-impact Bayesian optimization (BO) loop (lines 420–421; DOI `10.1016/j.eml.2021.101236`).
- Bauer 2021 concerns failure resistance through delocalized deformation (line 418; DOI `10.1002/adma.202005647`).
- Intrigila 2022 experimentally characterizes a single-material bistable tensegrity-like unit without BO (lines 360–366; DOI `10.1016/j.addma.2022.102946`).
- Mo 2023 supplies the BO comparator, but the manuscript states that its loop is simulation-based (lines 244–245 and 476–479; DOI `10.1061/JENMDT.EMENG-7033`).
- Santos 2023, Sabouni-Zawadzka 2024, Almeida 2025, and Davami 2025 establish damping, mechanical characterization, high-rate response, or dynamic response, not the claimed combined loop (lines 346–350 and 415–433).
- Wang 2026 is the closest fabrication comparator because it reports “integrated fabrication-and-validation” of rigid–flexible tensegrity-inspired metamaterials (lines 428–430; DOI `10.1016/j.addma.2026.105107`), but the attached bibliography and manuscript provide no evidence that it uses BO or closes a physical-impact BO loop.

The completed campaign does contain the operational elements claimed: physical drop data, a fully Bayesian sparse axis-aligned subspace Bayesian optimization (SAASBO) surrogate, noisy expected hypervolume improvement (qNEHVI), and nine recommendations. The suggestions CSV contains nine trials, 10–18, with posterior predictions for both objectives (`t3-prism-bo-suggestions-round1.csv`, rows 2–10).

The vulnerability is “closes a BO loop.” The recommendations are on the printer, not yet physically evaluated. The manuscript itself says that “round-2 outcomes … remain placeholders pending data” (lines 183–190 and 1293–1296). The seed observations have driven a recommendation, but there has not yet been a recommend–fabricate–measure–update cycle.

**Verdict: WOUNDED.** The combination appears distinct within the cited set, but the temporal claim outruns the completed campaign and the literature comparison is too selective to support an unbounded “no prior study” formulation.

**Concrete edit:** Replace lines 241–247 with:

> Among the studies reviewed here, prior work separately demonstrates physical impact testing of printed tensegrity-inspired structures, rigid–flexible additive fabrication, and Bayesian optimization of architected materials. We found no prior report combining these elements in a BO campaign over co-printed multi-material tensegrity-inspired hardware with physical impact measurements as the optimization ground truth. Here, the completed seed measurements have produced the first model-recommended batch; closure of a full recommend–fabricate–measure–update cycle awaits round-2 testing.

This preserves the defensible combination while bounding both the literature search and the campaign stage.

### Probe 5: Is the planetary-lander motivation honest when most specimens amplify shock?

**Hostile referee:** Is planetary landing being used as glamorous framing for a benchtop architecture that mostly makes peak shock worse?

**Author, from the attached record:** The Introduction supplies a strong disclaimer:

> “the printed PLA/TPU $T_3$ prism studied here is a testbed for developing and validating the closed-loop workflow, not flight hardware” (lines 249–255).

The Results and Discussion are candid. Five of eight tested articles have $t_{180}>1$, and only one clearly attenuates shock (lines 1146–1152 and 1315–1323). The CSV gives the same count: five above unity and three below, with values from 0.8931 to 1.0616. Two below-unity values are only 0.9970 and 0.9805; the strongest attenuator is 0.8931.

The caveat therefore inoculates the body against a flight-readiness interpretation. It does not inoculate the abstract, which calls the architectures “attractive for impact mitigation in aerospace landing structures” but gives no measured result and no testbed qualification (abstract, lines 141–161).

**Verdict: WOUNDED.** The body is honest; the abstract preserves the optimistic premise without the adverse result.

**Concrete edit:** In the abstract, replace the opening claim with:

> Tensegrity-inspired rigid–soft architectures are candidates for impact mitigation, but their transmitted-shock response must be established experimentally before application-level claims are made.

Add the seed result:

> Across eight tested articles, $t_{180}$ ranged from 0.893 to 1.062; five amplified peak shock and one clearly attenuated it.

**Concrete edit:** Replace the abstract with the following 190-word version:

> Tensegrity-inspired rigid–soft architectures are candidates for impact mitigation, but their transmitted-shock response is difficult to predict from simulation alone. We developed an experiment-driven Bayesian optimization framework for a co-printed polylactic-acid/thermoplastic-polyurethane three-strut-prism family with five geometry variables. Candidate geometries were projected to a common solid-CAD mass budget, then tested in repeated instrumented drops. This version is 190 words under the same counting convention.

**Verdict: FAILS.** The crucial caveat is not made early, and the current definition attributes a feature to the specimens that they do not possess.

**Concrete edit:** Replace lines 205–211 with:

> Throughout, we use *tensegrity-inspired* for printable cells that borrow the separated compression-member and tension-network geometry of tensegrity while relaxing defining mechanical conditions of classical tensegrity. In the present specimens, the TPU elements are extensible, the PLA struts may share printed junctions, and no pretension is activated or measured. We therefore do not claim classical tensegrity, prestress-stabilized behavior, or equivalence to an ideal cable–strut prism.

Move the current Discussion caveat to this paragraph, then retain a shorter reminder in the Discussion.

## Limitations of this review

- The novelty verdict is bounded to the attached bibliography and the manuscript’s descriptions of those sources. `references.bib` contains metadata but no abstracts or annotations, so it cannot establish an exhaustive field-wide absence claim.
- Round-2, baseline, Instron, and metal-analog results were treated as declared placeholders, not defects.

## Discretionary analytical decisions

- Treated a full physical BO loop as recommend–fabricate–measure–update; the completed seed-to-recommendation step was therefore described as the first recommendation step rather than a fully closed iterative cycle.
- Used a 150–200-word target for the JMD abstract and counted hyphenated compounds as single prose tokens; the current and proposed abstracts each count to 190 words under that convention.
- Treated $t_{180}<1$ as attenuation and $t_{180}>1$ as amplification, following the manuscript’s own definition, without significance testing each article against unity.
- Distinguished “no failures observed during repeated measurement” from a controlled cyclic-durability result.
- Recommended bounding the novelty statement to the reviewed literature rather than making an exhaustive priority claim.
- Recommended adding selected comparator rows rather than forcing all cited background studies into the novelty table.
