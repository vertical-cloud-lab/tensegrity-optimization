The main decision-changing findings are:

- A’s CFC-1000 instability is real but strongly trended. Its corrected T rises from 1.012 to 1.251 across the block. B is more stable than A in that narrow sense, but minimum variance does not establish geometry sensitivity.
- Arrangement is represented by one fixed-order block per condition. The ten drops are repeated observations, not ten independent arrangement replicates. The reported Welch and variance-test p-values are not design-valid.

**Cheapest risk-reduction experiment:** a randomized 20-drop crossover using two existing, distinct geometries on A and B, five drops per geometry-stack combination, with a common trigger, at least 2 ms pre-trigger, 50–100 ms post-impact, and prespecified input-conditioned shock response spectrum or band-transfer outcomes.
