### Probe 3: Are the claimed span, amplification count, and design-driven spread numerically defensible?

**Hostile referee:** Your prose appears to move among “seven of eight amplify,” a 17% spread, and a 19% spread. Which claims are actually in the manuscript, and which are supported by the data?

**Answer:** The premise needs correction. The attached manuscript does **not** claim that seven of eight amplify. It says:

> “measured $t_{180}$ spans 0.893 to 1.062: five of eight articles amplify … two sit marginally below unity (0.997 and 0.980), and a single design … attenuates it by 11%” (`manuscript-body.tex`, lines 1146–1150).

The CSV gives:

- minimum: 6lhxfy, $t_{180}=0.8930778$;
- maximum: bag26v, $t_{180}=1.0616198$;
- range: $0.1685420$, reported correctly as 0.169;
- five values above 1: 9hhbkp, autv5r, bag26v, bpx68c, and nvxsrv;
- three below 1: 6lhxfy, 6nheas, and amdjwm.

The manuscript’s “19% relative to the minimum” is also correct:

$$\frac{1.0616198-0.8930778}{0.8930778}\times100=18.87\%\approx19\%.$$

A 17% figure is obtained only by dividing the range by the eight-article mean:

$$\frac{0.1685420}{\bar t_{180}}\times100=16.79\%\approx17\%.$$

No 17% claim appears in the attached manuscript. The chosen 19% denominator is explicitly identified, so it is not misleading. The within-article coefficients of variation are 0.169%–0.482%, supporting the stated “0.2 to 0.5%” after rounding, although the minimum rounds to 0.17%, not 0.2%, at two decimals.

**Verdict: WITHSTANDS.** The manuscript’s actual claims, five of eight and 19% relative to the minimum, match the CSV. The requested “seven of eight” and “17%” checks refer to claims not present in this version.

### Probe 8: Can the surrogate-audit numbers be independently reproduced from the attachments?

**Hostile referee:** You report leave-one-out MAPE 2.6%, $r=0.70$, and rebound $r=-0.12$. Where are the seven held-out predictions needed to verify those statistics?

**Answer:** The attached LOOCV figure prints:

- $t_{180}$: “MAPE 2.6%   $r=0.70$”;
- rebound energy: “MAPE 37.8%   $r=-0.12$”;
- “Leave-one-out: each article predicted by a model that refit NUTS without it”; and
- “$n=7$.”

These labels agree with Section 4.2 (`manuscript-body.tex`, lines 1248–1255), and seven labels are shown in each panel. The signs also agree; this matters because PDF text extraction elsewhere renders the rebound value once as positive 0.12, while the supplied image clearly shows $-0.12$.

The magnitudes are plausible for seven points. If treated as ordinary Pearson correlations, $r=0.70$ implies $t(5)=2.19$, $p=0.080$, with a Fisher 95% interval of approximately $[-0.11,0.95]$; $r=-0.12$ implies $t(5)=-0.27$, $p=0.798$, with interval approximately $[-0.80,0.70]$. These intervals show why the manuscript should not present either correlation as stable.

However, I cannot independently recompute MAPE or either correlation from the attached numerical files. The CSV contains observations but not the seven held-out predictions or posterior summaries, and the source LOOCV data/code are absent. Reading approximate marker locations from a PNG is not an adequate numerical audit. The manuscript itself defers implementation details to campaign code, and the figure states that each fold refit No-U-Turn Sampling (NUTS), but the auditable fold-level output is not attached.

**Verdict: FAILS.** The prose matches the figure, and the results are plausible, but the three statistics are not independently reproducible from the supplied numerical record.

**Concrete edit and artifact change:** Export and archive a seven-row LOOCV CSV with at least

`article_id, objective, observed, predicted_mean, predicted_sd, fold_seed, model_specification`

and state whether $r$ is Pearson’s correlation. Then revise the paragraph to avoid “captures the ordering usefully,” which is too strong at $n=7$ and $p\approx0.08$:

> “Leave-one-out predictions for $t_{180}$ had MAPE 2.6% and Pearson $r=0.70$ across seven articles, but the correlation is imprecise at this sample size. Rebound-energy predictions had MAPE 37.8% and $r=-0.12$, providing no evidence of out-of-sample predictive skill.”

The main text should also report the rebound MAPE of 37.8%, already visible in the figure. Omitting it from the paragraph hides the most interpretable measure of prediction error.
