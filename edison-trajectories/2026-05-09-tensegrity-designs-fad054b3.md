# Comprehensive Literature Survey of Tensegrity Structure Designs

## 1. Introduction

Tensegrity ("tensional integrity") structures comprise isolated compression members (struts/bars) suspended within a continuous network of tension members (cables/tendons), achieving self-equilibrium through internal prestress. Since Kenneth Snelson's 1948 X-piece sculpture and R. Buckminster Fuller's coinage of the term, tensegrity has evolved from art into a rich engineering paradigm spanning architecture, robotics, metamaterials, biology, and space exploration (malai2024tensegritystructurethe pages 41-47, micheletti2022seventyyearsof pages 3-6). This survey systematically catalogs the major tensegrity design families described in the academic, patent, and grey literature, and concludes with specific recommendations for a BYU multi-material 3D-printing project.

## 2. Canonical Snelson-Class Prismatic Tensegrities

### 2.1 Three-Bar Prism (T3 / Simplex)
The simplest true tensegrity is the 3-bar prismatic simplex (T3): 3 struts, 9 cables (minimal) or 12 cables (non-minimal), and 6 nodes. Two triangular end-polygons are connected by struts with a characteristic stable twist angle θ = π/2 − π/n; for n = 3, θ = 30° (shaikh2026dynamicanalysisand pages 1-2, oliveira2009tensegritysystems pages 15-40). The T3 possesses one independent infinitesimal mechanism and one independent self-stress state; the mechanism is a combined vertical translation and rotation of the bases, stabilized by the self-stress (intrigila2022fabricationandexperimental pages 4-4). Oliveira and Skelton present this as the foundational "regular minimal tensegrity prism," noting that non-minimal variants with 12 strings provide higher stiffness and eliminate soft modes (oliveira2009tensegritysystems pages 15-40).

### 2.2 Four-Bar Prism (T4 / 4-Strut Simplex)
The 4-strut simplex consists of 4 struts and 12 cables arranged in an 8-node prism with one base rotated 135° relative to the other. Sabouni-Zawadzka and Gilewski classify it as a "quasi-bimode" extreme metamaterial unit cell, featuring stiff, soft, and medium extensional modes plus high and low shear modes. The self-stress level and cable-to-strut stiffness ratio serve as primary tuning parameters (sabounizawadzka2019softandstiff pages 3-4, sabounizawadzka2019softandstiff pages 1-3).

### 2.3 Higher-Order Prisms and Stacked Columns
The n-bar prism family generalizes to T6 (θ = 60°) and beyond. Prism twist angles cluster near 30–36° with cable pretension in the 1.8–2.5% engineering strain range for typical designs (shaikh2026dynamicanalysisand pages 1-2). Snelson's iconic Needle Tower I (1968) and Needle Tower II (1969) are composed by stacking smaller tensegrity prisms to form columnar masts (micheletti2022seventyyearsof pages 3-6). Yildiz and Lesieutre developed effective continuum beam stiffness properties for n-strut cylindrical tensegrity towers, enabling homogenized analysis of long stacked-prism columns for space applications.

## 3. Polyhedral / Spherical Tensegrities

### 3.1 Six-Strut Tensegrity Icosahedron
The 6-strut tensegrity icosahedron (also called Jessen's orthogonal icosahedron) is the canonical spherical tensegrity: 6 struts and 24 cables. This topology forms the basis of the NASA SUPERball robot, where the 2015 prototype featured 1.7 m struts, ~21 kg mass, 24 spring-cable assemblies, and 12 BLDC actuators (sabelhaus2015systemdesignand pages 1-2, sunspiral2015superballbotstructures pages 80-83). A payload variant adds 12 inner spring-cable assemblies for payload suspension (sunspiral2015superballbotstructures pages 29-32).

### 3.2 Expanded Octahedron
The expanded octahedron tensegrity has been studied as a metamaterial unit cell exhibiting three stiff modes, one purely soft mode, and two quasi-soft modes, with the largest positive-definite elasticity region among five compared regular tensegrity modules (sabounizawadzka2020extrememechanicalproperties pages 15-17). Vangelatos et al. fabricated expanded-octahedron-based bistable lattices using multiphoton lithography at nanoscale (minimum strut radii ~250 nm), demonstrating preserved twisting mechanisms under cyclic loading (vangelatos2020designandtesting pages 1-3).

### 3.3 Truncated Tetrahedron
The truncated tetrahedron module is identified as the most "extreme" regular tensegrity unit cell, featuring three purely stiff and three purely soft deformation modes (classified as a "trimode" material). However, its required self-stress and stiffness ranges lie beyond current practical fabrication limits (sabounizawadzka2020extrememechanicalproperties pages 15-17, sabounizawadzka2020extrememechanicalproperties pages 1-3).

### 3.4 Truncated Octahedron (Rimoli Cell)
The truncated octahedron tensegrity cell, introduced by Rimoli and Pal, has 12 struts and 36 cables and is inherently space-tileable. This cell is the foundation of the most important recent work on tensegrity metamaterials. Bauer et al. demonstrated that recursive reflection of truncated-octahedron cells into 2×2×2 and larger supercells creates metamaterials with isolated compression-member loops, suppressing localized failure and achieving up to 25-fold enhancement in deformability and orders-of-magnitude increases in energy absorption relative to same-strength conventional lattice architectures (bauer2021tensegritymetamaterialstoward pages 1-2). Pajunen et al. developed a 3D-printable, single-material equivalent that reproduces comparable strain-energy capacity with post-buckling stability and load-limiting behavior, validated through both quasi-static compression and drop-weight impact experiments (pajunen2019designandimpact pages 1-2).

## 4. Class-k Tensegrities

Skelton defines a "class-k" tensegrity as one in which at most k rigid bodies share a node. Class-1 tensegrities have no strut-to-strut connections (the canonical definition), while higher classes permit shared nodes (oliveira2009tensegritysystems pages 47-62, oliveira2009tensegritysystems pages 98-111).

### 4.1 T-Bar and D-Bar Units
The **T-bar** unit replaces a single compression bar of length ℓ₀ with an internally tensegritized subassembly: in the cited example, 4 bars and 16 strings forming a class-4 tensegrity. Mass reduction occurs when the internal angle α₁ < 45° (oliveira2009tensegritysystems pages 47-62). The **D-bar** is the dual of the T-bar, obtained by exchanging bars and strings; it is a class-2 tensegrity with string-to-string connections (oliveira2009tensegritysystems pages 47-62). These building blocks enable Skelton's minimum-mass optimization framework.

### 4.2 Minimum-Mass Columns and Plates
Skelton and de Oliveira derive closed-form optimal mass expressions for class-2 tensegrity cylinders parameterized by radius R, half-bay height z, helix angle φ, and complexity q. The normalized mass J = Js + Jb is minimized under material yield constraints, with optimal complexity q* balancing structural efficiency against joint mass penalties (oliveira2009tensegritysystems pages 98-111). For any given aspect ratio ρ and complexity q, the optimal bending angle satisfies tan β* = ρ^(1/q), with asymptotic minimal cost J*∞ = 2 ln ρ⁻¹ as q → ∞ (oliveira2009tensegritysystems pages 47-62).

## 5. Tensegrity Domes and Cable-Domes

### 5.1 Geiger Cable-Dome (Seoul Olympic Halls)
David Geiger adapted Fuller's Aspension Dome concept into radial cable-and-strut domes for the Seoul Olympic Gymnastic and Fencing Arenas (1986/1988). These are technically "cable-domes" rather than pure tensegrities because the outer compression ring is not embedded within the cable network (fu2005structuralbehaviorand pages 1-4, micheletti2022seventyyearsof pages 3-6). Micheletti and Podio-Guidugli trace the lineage from Fuller's bicycle-wheel-like arrangement through Geiger's implementation (micheletti2022seventyyearsof pages 3-6).

### 5.2 Levy Cable-Dome (Georgia Dome)
Levy revised Geiger's radial layout with a triangulated cable network for the Georgia Dome (1992). Fu's nonlinear FE analysis showed that the primary failure mode is slackening of ridge and diagonal cables in the central section, with recommendations to increase prestress in inner cables for enhanced capacity (fu2005structuralbehaviorand pages 4-9, fu2005structuralbehaviorand pages 9-13). The Georgia Dome used φ5 mm high-strength tensile wires (1670 N/mm²) and Q345 circular tube struts (345 N/mm²) under 0.6 kN/m² uniform roof load (fu2005structuralbehaviorand pages 4-9).

## 6. Bio-Inspired and Morphologically Novel Tensegrities

### 6.1 Biotensegrity (Levin, Ingber)
Levin's biotensegrity framework models musculoskeletal structures—spine, shoulder, pelvis—as "continuous tension, discontinuous compression" systems where bones act as compressive struts within a tensioned soft-tissue network (levin2007chapter15– pages 9-9, scarr2020biotensegritywhatis pages 1-2). Ingber's cellular tensegrity model demonstrates that cells use tensegrity architecture at the molecular level: actin-myosin filaments generate contractile tension (prestress), microtubules serve as compression struts, and intermediate filaments act as guy wires (swanson2013biotensegrityaunifying pages 4-6, ingber2008tensegritybasedmechanosensingfrom pages 5-7, ingber2008tensegritybasedmechanosensingfrom pages 9-10, ingber2014tensegritycellularbiophysics pages 1-3). This hierarchical principle operates across scales from single proteins to whole organisms, with mechanotransduction enabled by long-range force transmission through the tensegrity network (swanson2013biotensegrityaunifying pages 6-8, swanson2013biotensegrityaunifying pages 2-4).

### 6.2 Tensegrity Robots
**NASA SUPERball v1/v2**: A 6-strut icosahedron robot for planetary exploration, featuring 24 spring-cable assemblies and 12 actuators. The v1 (2015) measured ~1.7 m diameter, ~21 kg, with passive cable stiffness ~998 N/m and nominal actuated tension ~250 N (sabelhaus2015systemdesignand pages 1-2, sunspiral2015superballbotstructures pages 80-83). SUPERball v2 was redesigned to survive impact velocities upward of 8 m/s with 24 actuators and compliant nylon cables (up to 15% stretch). The **NASA Tensegrity Robotics Toolkit (NTRT)** is an open-source simulator built on the Bullet Physics engine, released for rapid exploration of tensegrity morphologies and control strategies (sabelhaus2014hardwaredesignand pages 1-2, sabelhaus2015systemdesignand pages 2-3).

**Berkeley ULTRA-Spine**: A flexible actuated spine robot using stacked tensegrity vertebra modules, controlled via model-predictive control with inverse statics optimization (sunspiral2015superballbotstructures pages 29-32). The TT-3 robot from UC Berkeley is a modular, rod-centered 6-bar tensegrity with distributed actuation and control architecture.

## 7. Tensegrity Space Structures and Deployable Systems

### 7.1 Deployable Tensegrity Masts
Tibert and Pellegrino developed deployable tensegrity mast concepts using stacked Snelson-type prisms, investigating both strut-mode deployment for eight-stage masts and tendon-controlled strategies (micheletti2022seventyyearsof pages 21-23). Sultan and Skelton proposed cable-controlled deployment following equilibrium manifolds to maintain structural integrity throughout transformation (rhodebarbarigos2012anactivedeployable pages 34-37).

### 7.2 Deployable Antenna Patents
**US 6,441,801 B1** (Knight et al., 2002): A screw-motion-driven tensegrity antenna with a parallel-platform architecture consisting of upper hexagonal platform and lower hexagonal base connected by compression strut and tension tie leg pairs (US6441801B1 pages 2-9, US6441801B1 pages 9-10, US6441801B1 pages 1-2). **US 6,542,132 B2** (Stern, 2003): A deployable reflector antenna with tensegrity support architecture and mounting frame for improved specific mass and compact stowage (US6542132B2 pages 1-9, US6542132B2 pages 9-10).

### 7.3 Wave Generation Patent
**US 8,616,328 B2** (Daraio, 2013): A tensegrity apparatus composed of multiple tensegrity units for transmitting solitary waves with adjustable profiles into materials or structures, enabling nondestructive evaluation applications (US8616328B2 pages 1-2).

## 8. Tensegrity-Inspired Metamaterials and Additive Manufacturing

### 8.1 Truncated Octahedron Lattices (Rimoli, Pajunen, Bauer)
The truncated-octahedron tensegrity cell (12 struts, 36 cables) is the leading architecture for 3D-printable impact-absorbing metamaterials. Pajunen et al. developed a single-material 3D-printable equivalent validated through ABAQUS simulations, quasi-static compression, and drop-weight impact tests (pajunen2019designandimpact pages 1-2). Bauer et al. achieved unprecedented failure resistance with up to 25-fold deformability enhancement and orders-of-magnitude increased energy absorption through delocalized deformation enabled by compression-member discontinuity (bauer2021tensegritymetamaterialstoward pages 1-2). Ruffini and Rimoli used graph theory to show that deformation delocalization correlates with asymmetric tension-compression connectivity, providing design principles for failure-resistant metamaterials.

### 8.2 Tunable Tensegrity Tessellations
Liu et al. developed a cuboctahedron tensegrity tessellation block (13 struts, 96 cables) with 12 independent prestress states and 17 internal mechanisms, using polymer-like material properties (E_S = 2600 MPa, E_C = 12 MPa) representative of multi-material 3D printing. Computational homogenization demonstrated rich tunability of effective stiffness and bandgaps (liu2019unravelingtensegritytessellations pages 7-9).

### 8.3 Bistable Tensegrity Units
Intrigila et al. fabricated bistable double-triangular-prism units via stereolithography, demonstrating snap-through behavior with preserved twisting mechanisms under cyclic loading (intrigila2022fabricationandexperimental pages 4-4, intrigila2022fabricationandexperimental pages 12-12). Vangelatos et al. extended this to nanoscale using multiphoton lithography with 250 nm minimum feature sizes (vangelatos2020designandtesting pages 1-3).

### 8.4 Simplex-Based Lattice Metamaterials
Sabouni-Zawadzka systematically compared five regular tensegrity unit cells—4-strut simplex (quasi-bimode), 3-strut simplex (quasi-trimode), expanded octahedron (quasi-trimode), truncated tetrahedron (trimode), and X-module (unimode)—identifying soft and stiff deformation modes via eigenanalysis of equivalent elasticity matrices (sabounizawadzka2020extrememechanicalproperties pages 15-17, sabounizawadzka2020extrememechanicalproperties pages 1-3). Self-stress level and cable-to-strut stiffness ratio are the primary tuning parameters, with six representative deformation modes: stiff, soft, and medium extensional, plus high (double) and low shear (sabounizawadzka2019softandstiff pages 1-3).

## 9. Topology Generation and Computational Design Methods

### 9.1 Form-Finding Methods
Tibert and Pellegrino's 2003 review established the standard taxonomy of form-finding approaches: force-density methods, energy minimization, dynamic relaxation, and analytical solutions for regular topologies (uzun2017formfindingandanalysis pages 1-2). Recent advances include genetic-algorithm-based total potential energy minimization for free-form tensegrities (Uzun 2017) and deep neural networks for form-finding that substitute trained DNNs for eigenvalue decomposition of force-density matrices (Lee et al. 2022). Graph neural networks have also been applied to tensegrity form-finding (Shao et al. 2024).

### 9.2 Open-Source Software
Key open-source tools include: **NTRT (NASA Tensegrity Robotics Toolkit)**, built on the Bullet Physics engine for tensegrity robot simulation (sabelhaus2014hardwaredesignand pages 1-2, sabelhaus2015systemdesignand pages 2-3); **MOTES (MOdeling TENsegrity Structures)**, a MATLAB-based open-source tool by Skelton's group for statics, dynamics, and form-finding of general tensegrity systems; and various research codes for computational homogenization of tensegrity metamaterials.

### 9.3 Elastic Lattice Patent for Tensegrity Robots
**US 2019/0382995 A1** (Chen, 2019): An elastic lattice platform for rapid prototyping of tensegrity structures and robots, where tensile members form a spatially defined structure without compressive members touching.

### Key Rationale and Parameter Recommendations

**Top Priority: Truncated Octahedron Cell (Rank 1).** This cell has the strongest experimental validation for impact absorption (pajunen2019designandimpact pages 1-2, bauer2021tensegritymetamaterialstoward pages 1-2). Its 12-strut/36-cable topology maps naturally to PETG compression members and TPU tension members. The Bambu H2D's multi-material capability can print PETG struts with TPU tendons in a single build, potentially eliminating post-assembly. Critical design variables for Bayesian optimization include: unit-cell edge length (5–20 mm), strut diameter (0.8–3 mm), TPU tendon diameter (0.4–1.5 mm), initial tendon prestrain (0–10%), node fillet radius, and lattice relative density (5–30%).

**Calibration Benchmark: T3 Simplex (Rank 2).** The 3-bar prism (3 struts, 9 cables, 6 nodes) is the ideal calibration geometry because it has a single self-stress state and single mechanism, enabling clean validation of PETG/TPU constitutive models and the prestress-printing workflow before scaling to more complex cells (oliveira2009tensegritysystems pages 15-40, intrigila2022fabricationandexperimental pages 4-4).

**Stacked Prismatic Column (Rank 7).** Particularly suited to the crutch-tip application because its axial geometry matches real crutch-tip aspect ratios. Progressive bay collapse/twisting under axial impact can absorb energy through multiple stages, and functionally graded stiffness along the column height can be achieved by varying bay parameters.

**Material-Specific Parameters for Bayesian Optimization:**
- **PETG struts**: diameter (0.8–4 mm), wall thickness if hollow (0.4–1.2 mm), length/slenderness ratio (critical for Euler buckling plateau)
- **TPU tendons**: durometer (80A–95A), cross-section (0.4–2 mm diameter), initial stretch/prestrain (0–15%)
- **Interface**: node geometry/fillet radius, PETG-TPU bonding area, overhang angle constraints
- **Lattice-level**: cell count per axis (2×2×2 to 6×6×6), grading function, boundary/skin conditions, overall relative density

**Open-Source Resources:** The NASA NTRT simulator (open-source, GitHub) provides validated physics for tensegrity dynamics. MOTES (MATLAB open-source by Skelton's group) handles statics and form-finding. For CAD generation, the parametric geometry of all recommended cells can be scripted in Python/OpenSCAD from published node coordinates. The Pajunen/Rimoli truncated-octahedron cell geometry is fully specified in the literature and reproducible for STL generation (pajunen2019designandimpact pages 1-2, liu2019unravelingtensegritytessellations pages 7-9).

## 11. Conclusion

This survey documents the remarkable breadth of tensegrity structural designs spanning seven decades—from Snelson's original prismatic sculptures through NASA's SUPERball planetary lander to Rimoli's failure-resistant metamaterials. For the BYU multi-material 3D-printing project, the truncated-octahedron tensegrity-inspired cell emerges as the highest-priority starting geometry due to its validated impact performance, space-tileability, and natural compatibility with PETG-strut/TPU-tendon multi-material fabrication (pajunen2019designandimpact pages 1-2, bauer2021tensegritymetamaterialstoward pages 1-2). The 3-bar simplex provides the ideal calibration benchmark (oliveira2009tensegritysystems pages 15-40, intrigila2022fabricationandexperimental pages 4-4), while the 4-strut simplex and expanded octahedron offer rich intermediate complexity for parametric exploration (sabounizawadzka2020extrememechanicalproperties pages 15-17, sabounizawadzka2019softandstiff pages 3-4). Bistable double-prism units add snap-through energy absorption capabilities particularly suited to crutch-tip applications (intrigila2022fabricationandexperimental pages 4-4). The stacked prismatic column geometry most naturally matches the axial loading profile of a crutch tip (shaikh2026dynamicanalysisand pages 1-2, micheletti2022seventyyearsof pages 3-6). All recommended geometries have sufficient published geometric specifications for parametric CAD generation, and the rich design variable space—spanning strut/tendon dimensions, prestrain, cell count, grading, and material selection—is well-suited to Bayesian optimization for systematic performance improvement.
