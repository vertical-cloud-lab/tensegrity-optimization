## 1. Method families and what matters for BO of energy-absorbing multi-material tensegrities

### 1.1 Reduced-order tensegrity models (rigid struts + axial tendons)
Rigid-bar + cable/spring models are common in tensegrity robotics because they are fast and can represent the dominant prestress-driven mechanics with relatively few degrees of freedom. NTRT, for example, uses Bullet rigid-body dynamics with custom spring–damper “cables,” actuated by changing rest length, and assigns friction/restitution to rods (mirletz2015towardsbridgingthe pages 1-2, iscen2014multiagentlearningfor pages 120-125). Such models are well suited for broad parametric sweeps and BO inner loops when (i) tendons are well approximated as axial force elements and (ii) tendon contact with struts/ground is not critical.

However, they generally cannot represent: (a) TPU hyperelasticity/viscoelasticity and hysteresis, (b) tendon self-contact and stick–slip friction with struts, or (c) impact/wave propagation within the strut material; these require continuum/beam formulations or data-driven correction terms. Reality-gap issues are explicitly noted in tensegrity robotics, including sensitivity to modeling assumptions and the lack of cable-ground contact in many simulators (wang2022closingthereality pages 115-119, mirletz2015towardsbridgingthe pages 1-2).

### 1.2 Form-finding and equilibrium (force density / optimization)
Force Density Method (FDM) and related static form-finding techniques are useful as geometry generators/initialization and for inverse kinematics of cable nets and tensegrities; the DuCTT work formulates inverse kinematics with FDM via pseudoinverse plus an optimization (minimizing cable energy) under nonnegative tension constraints, with simplifying assumptions such as rigid bars and frictionless ball joints (friesen2014ducttatensegrity pages 2-4). These methods are differentiable-friendly and can seed higher-fidelity dynamic simulations.

### 1.3 Continuum/beam FEM and contact-safe dynamics
Energy absorption in polymer lattices is strongly influenced by finite-strain constitutive behavior, viscoelasticity, contact/self-contact, and rate effects. For design optimization (including BO, which benefits from stable and robust forward solves), modern contact-safe approaches include incremental potential contact (IPC) barrier formulations (Li et al. 2020) and differentiable FEM solvers built around IPC. A key recent development is a differentiable solver for time-dependent deformation problems with contact and friction, combining FEM + high-order time integration with IPC and providing efficient analytic adjoints for parameters including shape, material, friction, and initial conditions (huang2024differentiablesolverfor pages 1-2, huang2024differentiablesolverfor pages 2-3). These families are much better aligned with self-contacting lattices and tendon/strut interactions than classic penalty-only contact.

### 1.4 Differentiable simulation engines and learned surrogates
Differentiable simulators can reduce the number of expensive evaluations needed for calibration/inverse design and can be embedded inside gradient-based substeps even when the outer loop is BO. DiffPD is a prominent differentiable projective-dynamics FEM simulator with penalty-based and complementarity-based contact options, and reported 4–19× speedups versus Newton methods in benchmarked tasks (du2021diffpddifferentiableprojective pages 1-2, du2021diffpddifferentiableprojective media 76462e9c). IPC-based differentiable FEM provides an alternative emphasis: robust handling of complex geometry contact with small overhead for adjoints (often <10% over forward) (huang2024differentiablesolverfor pages 1-2).

For tensegrity robotics specifically, first-principles differentiable engines and learned GNN simulators have been used to reduce sim-to-real gap and/or create efficient differentiable models. Wang (2022) introduces a recurrent differentiable tensegrity engine with implicit integration and differentiable collision checking; it is trained to match MuJoCo ground truth and is data-efficient for policy transfer (wang2022arecurrentdifferentiable pages 1-2, wang2022arecurrentdifferentiable pages 2-3). Chen et al. (2024) propose a learned GNN simulator over the tensegrity graph and report improved accuracy versus the analytical differentiable engine in some settings, with MuJoCo as simulation ground truth and additional real 3-bar trajectory evaluation (chen2024learningdifferentiabletensegrity pages 1-3).

### 2.1 NASA Tensegrity Robotics Toolkit (NTRT)
**Formulation.** NTRT is built on Bullet Physics v2.82 and uses a custom Hooke’s-law spring–damper cable model rather than Bullet’s default position-based soft-body strings. Cables are actuated by changing rest length under speed/acceleration/force limits; rods carry friction/restitution parameters; models are built with builder tools from node coordinates and connectivity (mirletz2015towardsbridgingthe pages 1-2). NTRT runs with fixed timesteps (1000 Hz in the cited work) and can run faster than real time depending on contact complexity (mirletz2015towardsbridgingthe pages 1-2, mirletz2016adaptivecentralpattern pages 42-47).

**Contact/large deformation.** Rigid-body contact is handled via Bullet, but in the described implementation cables were not collision objects; the authors report they were implementing contact dynamics for cables (mirletz2015towardsbridgingthe pages 1-2). This limitation is important for energy-absorbing tensegrity lattices where TPU tendons may self-contact or wrap around struts.

**Validation.** NTRT includes experimental validation: (i) prior kinematic validation on ReCTeR with ~1.3% position error and (ii) additional dynamic validation via direct comparison of simulated and measured forces on the Tetraspine robot (mirletz2015towardsbridgingthe pages 1-2). DuCTT also cites validation of NTRT accuracy against an Euler–Lagrange simulator and motion-capture experiments (friesen2014ducttatensegrity pages 2-4).

### 2.2 Differentiable tensegrity engines and learned simulators (2022–2024)
**First-principles differentiable engine (Wang 2022).** The recurrent differentiable tensegrity engine addresses a common obstacle: real trajectories are low-frequency, whereas contact events are high-frequency. It adds an implicit integration scheme, progressive training, and a differentiable collision checker, and uses MuJoCo as a ground-truth simulator for sim2sim training; it reports that successful transfer back to the ground-truth system can be achieved with ~100× less ground-truth data than training directly on the ground-truth simulator (wang2022arecurrentdifferentiable pages 1-2, wang2022arecurrentdifferentiable pages 2-3). The associated thesis emphasizes a remaining reality-gap contributor: many simulators treat cables as “virtual,” lacking cable-ground contact, which can cause failures on rough terrain (wang2022closingthereality pages 115-119).

**Learned GNN simulator (Chen 2024).** Uses a sparse graph representation aligned with tensegrity topology and is evaluated against MuJoCo and real 3-bar data; it highlights that analytical differentiable engines can be hard to train under partial observability (e.g., missing velocities), whereas the learned model can be warm-started in simulation and fine-tuned (chen2024learningdifferentiabletensegrity pages 1-3).

For BYU MRG energy-absorption design, these robotics-focused tensegrity simulators are most relevant as *fast, topology-level* priors or surrogate models; they do not replace continuum modeling for TPU hysteresis and plasticity in struts.

### 2.3 MOTES (Modeling of Tensegrity Structures)
MOTES provides static minimum-mass design (LP and nonlinear variants) and dynamic simulation based on second-order matrix differential equations assuming rigid bars and linearly elastic strings. It uses modified Runge–Kutta integration with bar-length correction and supports massive strings via discretization into point masses; it provides reduced-order constrained dynamics for class-k systems (goyal2019motesmodelingof pages 1-2). Contact/friction and experimental validation were not evidenced in the retrieved excerpts (goyal2019motesmodelingof pages 1-2).

### 2.4 MuJoCo / MJX (JAX-based GPU MuJoCo)
MuJoCo Playground demonstrates an integrated MJX (MuJoCo XLA) stack: a JAX-based branch of MuJoCo enabling GPU on-device simulation and training, with an end-to-end pipeline supporting sim-to-real transfer across multiple platforms, and emphasizing rapid iteration as a practical requirement (zakka2025mujocoplayground pages 1-2). While this highlights excellent *automation/performance* properties for BO/RL loops, the retrieved evidence does not establish soft-body/hyperelastic TPU tendon support; it is primarily a contact-rich rigid-body simulator in this context (zakka2025mujocoplayground pages 1-2, zakka2025mujocoplayground pages 2-4). MuJoCo is also used as ground truth in tensegrity sim2sim validation and differentiable-engine training (wang2022arecurrentdifferentiable pages 1-2).

### 2.5 PyBullet and Gazebo (simulation ecosystem choices)
A review of RL-oriented physics engines describes PyBullet as a Python wrapper over Bullet’s C-API supporting URDF/MJCF loading and ML framework integration, and Gazebo as a simulator framework that can switch among physics backends (ODE/Bullet/Simbody/DART) with rich sensor support (kaup2024areviewof pages 4-5). These sources support the claim that PyBullet/Gazebo are easy to script in Python for BO loops, but they do not provide evidence of hyperelastic TPU, viscoelasticity, or contact-safe deformable simulation suitable for high-fidelity energy absorption (kaup2024areviewof pages 4-5).

### 2.6 SOFA + FEniCSx coupling (SOniCS)
SOniCS is a plugin combining SOFA’s interactive scenegraph with FEniCSx’s UFL/FFCx-generated FE tensors and automatic differentiation of residuals/Jacobians, aiming to make it easy to test and swap constitutive models. It demonstrates hyperelastic contact (haptic simulation with a hyperelastic liver) and includes examples of Saint Venant–Kirchhoff, Neo-Hookean, Mooney–Rivlin, and Holzapfel–Ogden anisotropic models. It is verified with manufactured solutions, cantilever beams, and FEBio benchmarks (mazier2024sonicsdevelopintuition pages 1-2). This stack is relevant to TPU tendon hyperelastic modeling and to building surrogates from many simulations, but the retrieved evidence does not confirm high-rate impact/wave propagation or multi-material lattice fracture.

### 2.7 DiffPD (Differentiable Projective Dynamics FEM)
DiffPD implements FEM via projective dynamics with an implicit scheme and a global-local solver; it supports two contact approaches: penalty-based contact+friction and complementarity-based non-penetration/static friction, and accelerates backprop using prefactorized Cholesky factors. It reports 4–19× speedups over Newton-based approaches and scales to ~30k DoFs (du2021diffpddifferentiableprojective pages 1-2, du2021diffpddifferentiableprojective pages 2-3). A sim-to-real study using DiffPD benchmarks virtual robots (beam, pneumatic arm, fish tail), analyzes implicit-Euler numerical damping and proposes adjustments, and provides experimental comparisons (dubied2022simtorealforsoft pages 1-2). For polymer lattice energy absorption, DiffPD is attractive as a differentiable medium-fidelity simulator, but contact robustness for complex self-contacting lattice geometries is flagged in later comparative work as limited to simpler collision representations (planes/SDF) in some settings (huang2024differentiablesolverfor pages 2-3).

### 2.8 IPC-based differentiable FEM (PolyFEM + IPC)
The differentiable solver of Huang et al. (2024) combines FEM with a high-order time integrator and IPC contact/friction to support both static and dynamic problems and differentiate w.r.t. shape, material, friction, and initial conditions. It derives analytic adjoints with small overhead (often <10% for nonlinear problems) and emphasizes robustness with complex contact/self-contact geometry (huang2024differentiablesolverfor pages 1-2, huang2024differentiablesolverfor pages 2-3). For tensegrity lattices with tendon/strut contact and large deformations, this is among the best-evidenced choices for contact-robust, optimization-compatible simulation.

### 2.9 Jade (differentiable rigid-body contact)
Jade is a differentiable articulated rigid-body engine modeling frictional contact as an LCP, using continuous collision detection and backtracking to prevent interpenetration, and modifying Dantzig’s algorithm for correlated friction variables (yang2024jadeadifferentiable pages 1-2). It is relevant if BYU MRG pursues rigid-strut models plus rigorous frictional contact, but it is not a deformable TPU tendon solver in the evidenced excerpt.

## 4. Concrete recommendations for BYU MRG: simulation stacks for a multi-fidelity BO workflow

### Recommendation A (best overall for energy absorption with contact): IPC-based differentiable FEM + TPU material calibration
**Stack.** PolyFEM-based differentiable dynamics with IPC contact (Huang et al. 2024) as the high-fidelity core (huang2024differentiablesolverfor pages 1-2, huang2024differentiablesolverfor pages 2-3), paired with a TPU characterization model calibrated from lattice experiments (hyperelastic/viscoelastic/hysteretic material modeling is standard in FFF TPU lattice studies) (dubied2022simtorealforsoft pages 1-2).

**Why it fits.** Energy absorption in tensegrity lattices depends on large deformation plus tendon/strut and self-contact; IPC is specifically designed for robust intersection-free contact/friction on complex geometries and supports differentiation w.r.t. shape/material/friction, which reduces sample count and enables hybrid BO+gradient local refinement (huang2024differentiablesolverfor pages 1-2).

**Compute cost expectation.** Highest of the recommended options due to FEM + contact barrier; expect minutes per design for moderate meshes, potentially longer for high-resolution lattices and many contact pairs. However, adjoint overhead is reported small relative to forward solves (<10% in typical nonlinear cases), so sensitivity-enabled calibration/inverse steps are practical (huang2024differentiablesolverfor pages 1-2).

**Pitfalls.** Requires careful meshing and parameterization of multi-material geometry; constitutive models must capture TPU rate dependence and hysteresis (not guaranteed by default). Also, IPC-based methods can be sensitive to tangential force artifacts in some regimes (known in the IPC literature), so validation against drop/impact experiments is essential.

### Recommendation B (mid-fidelity differentiable, fast iteration): DiffPD for surrogate training + occasional IPC “truth” runs
**Stack.** DiffPD-based FEM as a medium-fidelity, differentiable simulator to generate many training points and to support rapid inverse design steps, with periodic calibration/validation runs in IPC-based FEM to correct contact/hysteresis mismatch (du2021diffpddifferentiableprojective pages 1-2, dubied2022simtorealforsoft pages 1-2, huang2024differentiablesolverfor pages 2-3).

**Why it fits.** DiffPD has strong evidence of speed (4–19× vs Newton baselines) and has been validated against experiments in soft robotic structures (beam/arm/fish tail), demonstrating a practical recipe for sim-to-real parameter tuning (dubied2022simtorealforsoft pages 1-2, du2021diffpddifferentiableprojective media 76462e9c). This supports BO loops that require many evaluations.

**Compute cost expectation.** Lower than IPC-based FEM and amenable to running hundreds–thousands of simulations for BO/surrogate fitting.

**Pitfalls.** Contact modeling may be less robust for complex, self-contacting 3D lattice geometries than IPC-based methods (huang2024differentiablesolverfor pages 2-3). Material model expressiveness may be limited relative to what’s needed for TPU visco-hyperelasticity and for plasticity/damage in PLA/PETG struts.

### Recommendation C (low-fidelity topology-level screening): NTRT-style rigid strut + tendon model (or MuJoCo/MJX rigid approximations)
**Stack.** Use a fast multibody tensegrity model (NTRT-like: rigid struts + spring–damper tendons) to screen topology, prestress, and gross stiffness/kinematics, then hand off promising designs to DiffPD/IPC FEM (mirletz2015towardsbridgingthe pages 1-2).

**Why it fits.** NTRT demonstrates fast simulation, builder-based model generation, and experimental validation for dynamic tensegrity robots (mirletz2015towardsbridgingthe pages 1-2). It can reduce the BO search space by cheaply rejecting designs that collapse or cannot be prestressed into stable configurations.

**Compute cost expectation.** Very low (seconds per run typical), enabling large BO candidate pools.

**Pitfalls.** Cables may lack contact modeling (not collision objects in cited implementation), and tendon hyperelasticity/viscoelasticity is not represented; thus energy absorption predictions will be unreliable without higher-fidelity refinement (mirletz2015towardsbridgingthe pages 1-2, wang2022closingthereality pages 115-119).

## 5. Practical BO workflow (multifidelity) consistent with the evidence
1. **Geometry + prestress initialization:** use FDM/form-finding (DuCTT-style) to generate equilibrium configurations satisfying nonnegative tension and geometric constraints (friesen2014ducttatensegrity pages 2-4).
2. **Low-fidelity screening:** rigid-strut + tendon model (NTRT-like) to quickly map the stability region and approximate stiffness envelope (mirletz2015towardsbridgingthe pages 1-2).
3. **Mid-fidelity sampling:** DiffPD for large batches, plus material/damping calibration recipes and experimental alignment guidance (dubied2022simtorealforsoft pages 1-2, du2021diffpddifferentiableprojective pages 1-2).
4. **High-fidelity confirmation + contact-critical behavior:** IPC-based differentiable FEM with shape/material/friction sensitivities (huang2024differentiablesolverfor pages 1-2).
5. **Surrogate + BO:** use multi-fidelity BO (e.g., trust-region BO with fidelity scheduling) where the acquisition function accounts for cost; incorporate gradients where available (DiffPD or IPC-based adjoints) to reduce uncertainty more efficiently.

## 6. Summary
For multi-material 3D-printed energy-absorbing tensegrity lattices with TPU tendons, the strongest evidence-supported path is a *contact-robust FEM core* (IPC-based differentiable FEM) augmented by a *faster differentiable mid-fidelity engine* (DiffPD) and a *fast multibody tensegrity screen* (NTRT-style) (huang2024differentiablesolverfor pages 1-2, dubied2022simtorealforsoft pages 1-2, mirletz2015towardsbridgingthe pages 1-2). MuJoCo/MJX provides excellent GPU-scale automation for rigid-body learning workflows, and is proven as a ground-truth engine for tensegrity dynamics model learning, but soft TPU tendon physics is not evidenced in the retrieved sources (zakka2025mujocoplayground pages 1-2, wang2022arecurrentdifferentiable pages 1-2).
