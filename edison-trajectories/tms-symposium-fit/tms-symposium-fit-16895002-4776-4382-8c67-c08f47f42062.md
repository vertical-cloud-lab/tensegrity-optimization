2. STRENGTHENING THE PROCESSING ANGLE

Yes, you can make this a defensible processing paper, but only if you do more than sprinkle in FDM buzzwords. You need at least one of these to be true:
- process parameters are part of the BO decision vector
- process-dependent interface quality / defect state is measured and modeled
- the workflow explicitly learns **processing → defects/interfaces → crash metrics**

For PLA+TPU multi-material FDM, the most plausible process parameters affecting your outcomes are below.

Most plausible parameters for **interfacial bond strength** at the rigid-flexible boundary:
- **Nozzle temperature** for PLA and TPU separately. This is probably the most defensible addition because interdiffusion, wetting, and local fusion quality depend on melt temperature and viscosity.
- **Print speed** near the interface. Slower deposition usually increases local thermal contact time and can improve bonding, though too slow can worsen sagging or overheat features.
- **Interface overlap / interlock settings** (overlap distance, co-deposition overlap, number of interface walls, or intentional interpenetration geometry if slicer/toolpath allows). This is probably the single best “processing-aware” knob if your machine/software supports it.
- **Flow/extrusion multiplier** for each material, especially at the interface. Under-extrusion promotes kissing bonds and voids; over-extrusion may improve contact but distort geometry.
- **Cooling fan setting**, especially right after deposition at the PLA/TPU boundary. Cooling rate changes wetting and interlayer/intermaterial adhesion.
- **Layer height**. This affects contact area, bead flattening, void morphology, and staircase-induced stress concentrations.
- **Line width / extrusion width**. Similar logic: changes bead geometry and actual contact area.

Parameters most plausible for **defect populations** and print feasibility:
- **Layer height**
- **Print speed**
- **Flow multiplier**
- **Retraction settings**, especially if TPU stringing or underfill corrupts the flexible network
- **Cooling**
- **Bed temperature**, mostly for warpage/adhesion and dimensional consistency, more relevant to print success than directly to energy absorption
- **Nozzle switching / purge / wipe strategy**, if your system uses a tool-change or shared-nozzle multimaterial setup

Parameters most plausible for **energy absorption metrics** indirectly through structure quality:
- **Interface overlap / bond-promoting settings** because interface failure will change force plateau behavior and compaction mode
- **Nozzle temperature** because it changes bond strength and defect state
- **Layer height** because it affects anisotropy, void fraction, and buckling/collapse progression
- **Print speed** because it affects filament placement fidelity and bonding
- **Flow multiplier** because it affects density, bead fusion, and local stiffness

Parameters I would *not* add unless you truly have data and a mechanism:
- Bed temperature as a headline variable for energy absorption. It matters for print quality and adhesion to the bed, but it is weaker as a direct scientific story for absorber mechanics.
- Retraction as a main variable unless print failures/stringing are a major limitation in your TPU path.
- Generic “all slicer settings” language. That reads like fishing.

What I’d recommend adding if you want a credible processing-aware-ML version:

Best minimal processing variable set:
- **PLA nozzle temperature**
- **TPU nozzle temperature**
- **print speed** or **interface-region print speed**
- **layer height**
- **interface overlap / interpenetration setting** if available

If that’s too many for a first pass, the most defensible 2–3 are:
- **interface overlap**
- **TPU and/or PLA nozzle temperature**
- **layer height**

Why these are defensible:
- They have a direct mechanism for changing bond quality and defect morphology.
- They are easy for reviewers to recognize as real process variables.
- They connect naturally to both manufacturability and mechanical response.

What to measure so the framing is credible:
- Existing crash metrics: peak transmitted force, specific energy absorption, compaction efficiency
- **Print success/failure** rate, already in your loop
- **Interfacial failure mode** during compression: did the TPU detach, did the PLA fracture, did delamination dominate?
- **As-built density / mass error** relative to nominal
- **Dimensional error** in struts and tension elements
- **Void/defect proxies** from optical microscopy, XCT, or at least cross-sectional imaging near the interface
- **Interfacial bond strength proxy**, ideally from a small coupon test or pull-off / lap-shear / peel-like benchmark tailored to your co-printed geometry

Strongest processing-aware framing:
- “We optimize both architecture and selected process parameters because multimaterial FDM interface formation and defect populations materially alter absorber performance.”
- “The model learns a processing–defect/interface–property relationship, not just a geometry–property relationship.”
- “Failed prints and weak interfaces are treated as manufacturability constraints.”

Weak framing to avoid:
- “Processing-aware” with no process variables in the search space
- mentioning microstructure unless you are actually characterizing something that reasonably stands in for microstructure/morphology/interface state in polymers

For polymers/FDM, I would use **“as-built morphology, interfacial bonding, and defect state”** more than “microstructure” unless you truly mean semicrystalline morphology or mesostructure and have data.

A credible revised abstract direction for the current symposium would be something like:
- BO over architecture + a compact set of FDM process variables
- measured crash metrics + manufacturability + interface quality proxy
- emphasis on process-dependent interfacial fusion and defect control in PLA/TPU multimaterial FDM
