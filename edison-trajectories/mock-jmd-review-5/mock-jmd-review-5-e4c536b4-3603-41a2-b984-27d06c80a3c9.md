Mock Reviewer #1 -- Design / Mechanical Engineering (ASME JMD)

Recommendation: Major Revision

This round is better than round 4. The newly integrated tensegrity-additive-manufacturing literature closes the specific reference gap that remained in the prior AE letter, and the manuscript is now more credible in how it positions itself relative to published tensegrity-inspired printed structures. The paper is also structurally much tighter than earlier drafts: the problem statement, constrained multi-objective BO formulation, design-variable table, fixed-joint rationale, and scope limitations are substantially clearer. That said, the manuscript is still not yet publishable in JMD form because it remains a planned-methods paper with placeholder Results and Discussion, several synthetic figures, and incomplete fabrication/test parameter disclosure. My recommendation stays at Major Revision rather than moving to Minor Revision because the remaining issues are not cosmetic.

Major comments

1. Novelty is now defensible, but only if stated narrowly and consistently.
The revised background now appropriately cites Bauer et al. (2021), Pajunen et al. (2021), Sabouni-Zawadzka et al. (2024), Almeida et al. (2025), Davami et al. (2025), and Wang et al. (2026), and uses them mainly to establish that tensegrity-inspired architectures can be additively manufactured and can exhibit programmable and dynamic response. That is the right use of those papers. The manuscript should keep the novelty claim exactly at the level now implied by Table 1/novelty positioning: not “first tensegrity-inspired printed absorber,” not “first AM tensegrity impact study,” but the combination of (i) multi-material PLA/TPU FDM, (ii) this internal-anchor joint concept, and (iii) an experiment-driven BO loop trained directly on physical measurements. If the authors overstate beyond that, Reviewer 1-level pushback will return immediately.

The manuscript still contains an explicit TODO in the Introduction to “add 1–2 sentences clearly stating the gap this paper closes vs. Pajunen et al. and Mo et al.” That gap statement is needed in the final paper, because the current claim is spread across prose plus the novelty table rather than crisply stated in the main narrative.

2. The BO formulation is much improved, but some modeling choices still need tighter justification for a JMD audience.
The methods now clearly state a constrained multi-objective problem: maximize SEA and compaction efficiency subject to peak transmitted force F_max <= F*; use qNEHVI; anchor the hypervolume reference point at the rigid-control point; keep single-objective LogEI only as a baseline; hold robust/evolution-guided variants as contingency. This is coherent.

Still, several points need firmer justification in the final draft:
- Kernel: the Matérn-5/2 ARD GP is reasonable, but the manuscript should say why this kernel is preferred for this mechanical design problem over squared-exponential or other common choices.
- Noise: the model uses inferred homoscedastic noise. That is acceptable as a starting choice, but it may be fragile if print-to-print variability changes across geometry regimes. The authors should either justify why homoscedastic noise is adequate for the planned campaign or state clear criteria for escalating to heteroscedastic/replicate-aware treatment.
- SAASBO at approximately 5 variables: the current text says SAASBO is retained despite modest dimensionality because its shrinkage priors may improve uncertainty quantification at low sample size, and that this will be benchmarked against a standard single-task GP baseline. Good. But this is still unusual enough that the paper must report that benchmark clearly and not assume SAASBO is self-justifying.
- Batch/budget: n=9 Sobol + 10 batches x q=5 = 59 specimens is internally consistent. Good. But one TODO still says “confirm the final budget against printer throughput.” That cannot remain in a submitted paper.
- Reference point: using the rigid-control point as the qNEHVI hypervolume reference point is sensible and interpretable. Keep this. It is one of the stronger design choices in the current draft.

3. Parameterization is clearer, but Table 1 and the surrounding text need one more consistency pass.
The active continuous variables are R, H, theta, d_s, and d_t, with fixed d_j = 7.0 mm. That is now much better defined. The explanation for fixing d_j is also sound: it is constrained by outlet count and nozzle-clearance/joint-geometry considerations, and keeping it fixed avoids confounding energy-absorption performance with joint-strength changes. Good.

Still, I would ask the authors to check three things carefully:
- The text says the family is parameterized via four groups of variables, but only five continuous variables are active in the present campaign. That distinction is correct, but it should be restated once in plainer language for readers who do not work in BO.
- The manuscript says the surrogate will be fit on realized as-built geometry, not commanded CAD values. That is a strong choice. The authors should make explicit whether Table 1 ranges refer to commanded CAD inputs, realized measured values, or both.
- The as-built discretization paragraph is good, but it quietly undercuts the “continuous variable” framing. I think that is honest, but it means the final paper should explain how measured diameters are fed back into Ax/BoTorch without creating bookkeeping ambiguity between design intent and realized specimen.

4. Venue fit is plausible for JMD, but the paper must stay design-method centric.
As revised, this can fit JMD if the final manuscript centers on design representation, experiment-driven optimization, and the way the constrained multi-objective formulation guides the search. If the eventual paper leans mainly on materials processing, impact test traces, and print-parameter tuning, it drifts toward Additive Manufacturing or Smart Materials and Structures. Right now the framing still supports JMD, especially because the manuscript emphasizes parameterization, search strategy, and design tradeoffs, but the final Discussion will need to connect the results back to design methodology rather than only to test outcomes.

Minor comments

1. The manuscript still has placeholder/synthetic BO figures (LOOCV, sensitivity, convergence, Pareto). That is acceptable for internal drafting but not for submission. The captions are honest about this.

2. The “high-fidelity validation experiments using pretensioned tensegrity assemblies” contribution is not yet fully reconciled with the later “metal-analog validation” wording. It appears the intent is true-cable metal analogs with measured pretension, but the paper should state that directly and consistently.

3. The quasi-static standards paragraph cites several ASTM/ISO documents adapted to architected polymer cells. That is fine, but the final paper should separate what was actually followed from what was consulted.

4. The print-parameter table is still largely TBD. That is a hard gate for publication.

5. The out-of-scope statement is appropriate and should remain: off-axis/combined loading, multi-hit/cyclic durability, and landing-attitude variability are future work.

Editorial comments

1. Remove the Introduction TODO about clarifying the gap versus Pajunen and Mo by actually writing those sentences.
2. Remove all manuscript-status TODO text before submission.
3. Replace “to our knowledge” formulations with explicit scope-bounded claims where possible.
4. The novelty table is useful; consider renaming it from generic “Positioning of this work” to something closer to “Scope-relative novelty positioning.”
5. Make sure “Table 1” references are consistent with JMD production style after final typesetting.

Bottom line

This round is closer to publishable form than round 4. The literature-positioning problem is now largely fixed. The remaining barriers are not the high-level framing anymore; they are the lack of real results, incomplete process/test disclosure, and the need to show that the chosen BO machinery is not just fashionable but necessary and empirically supported for this search space.


Mock Reviewer #2 -- Biomechanics / Impact Mechanics

Recommendation: Major Revision

The manuscript is more disciplined than prior rounds about what it does and does not claim. I appreciate that the authors now state plainly that the present study is limited to single-cell, fixed-topology T3-prism specimens under axial quasi-static and drop-weight loading, and that off-axis loading, combined loading, multi-hit/cyclic durability, and landing-attitude variability are future work. That honesty helps.

My main concern is that the application motivation and the test configuration are still not fully aligned.

Major comments

1. The motivation is split between planetary landing and later crutch-tip translation, but the mechanical evidence chain is still thin.
The manuscript front end references planetary-landing tensegrity systems and “aerospace landing structures,” while the Discussion contains a future-work path toward crutch-tip miniaturization. That ordering is good in the sense that the crutch-tip concept is no longer driving the Introduction. Still, for a biomechanics/impact reader, the paper must decide what primary use-case the current axial test actually informs.

If the main motivation is planetary/payload landing, then the paper should explain more explicitly how the axial drop-tower observables map to that use-case. If the main motivation is eventual assistive-device cushioning, then the present fixture is even further removed from the real loading envelope. The current text sits in between.

2. The axial fixture is acceptable as a first screening test, but the manuscript should not imply more transferability than it has earned.
The paper now does a decent job limiting scope, but the drop protocol still uses a constrained specimen in a bungee-assisted drop tower where unconstrained specimens would otherwise lift off the base. That is a very specific apparatus-dependent loading scenario. It may still be useful for comparing candidate geometries under controlled axial events, but it is not a surrogate for realistic off-axis touchdown, repeated contacts, or user-device interactions.

I would want the final paper to say more explicitly that the current impact rig is a comparative screening platform, not a faithful recreation of the operational loading environment.

3. The metric mapping remains a critical issue: SEA is not the same thing as injury-relevant cushioning.
The revised BO formulation improves this by making peak transmitted force a hard constraint and optimizing SEA and compaction efficiency only within that admissible region. That is the right direction. It addresses one of the big issues from earlier rounds: high SEA alone can reward absorbers that still transmit unacceptable peaks.

Even so, the manuscript should explain in plain mechanics terms why the rigid-control-derived threshold F* is the right admissibility boundary. Is it a relative-improvement threshold only, or is it intended to approximate a use-case-based force ceiling? Right now it appears to be a control-relative threshold, which is methodologically okay for screening, but weaker as an application-specific claim.

4. The planned shock-processing language is better, but still needs real data and full protocol disclosure.
The manuscript properly cites SAE J211 for CFC-180 filtering and shows a synthetic mechanistic figure with impulse-consistent traces. Good conceptually. But until the actual sampling rate, sensor mounting, channel conditioning, drop heights, and event-window definitions are reported, the impact conclusions remain provisional.

5. The metal-analog transfer test is interesting but should be framed as exploratory, exactly as the current text says.
This is one of the stronger revised sections. The paper explicitly notes that deformation modes, joint compliance, friction, pretension behavior, and strain-rate sensitivity differ between PLA/TPU and Al/SS systems, and therefore agreement is not expected a priori. Good. Keep that language. The pre-specified Spearman rank-correlation criterion is also a sensible way to keep the claim ordinal rather than over-quantitative.

Minor comments

1. The planned Lansmont M23 replicate campaign for selected Pareto designs is a good addition. If completed, it could strengthen the dynamic-mechanics side substantially.

2. The manuscript should distinguish more clearly between what is measured directly in quasi-static compression and what is inferred or adapted for dynamic interpretation.

3. The phrase “out-cushion the uncushioned baseline” is intuitive but a bit informal for the final paper.

4. If the final paper keeps a planetary-landing motivation, consider adding one short paragraph linking the chosen metrics to the usual landing-system concerns: peak deceleration, impulse redistribution, stroke/compaction, and mass efficiency.

Bottom line

The manuscript has improved because the optimization target is now better matched to impact relevance than in earlier drafts. Still, the current study is a controlled axial screening framework, not yet a convincing application-level validation for either planetary landing or assistive-device biomechanics. That is fine, but the paper should say exactly that.


Mock Reviewer #3 -- Additive Manufacturing / Materials

Recommendation: Major Revision

The paper is more credible on the additive-manufacturing side than in prior rounds. The authors now use the newly integrated tensegrity-AM literature appropriately: not as proof of their exact junction/process concept, but as evidence that printed tensegrity-inspired architectures can be made and can show useful mechanical behavior. That is the right rhetorical move. I no longer see the major literature-positioning omission that was still open previously.

My remaining concerns are about processing evidence and manufacturability disclosure, not literature awareness.

Major comments

1. The PLA+TPU multi-material processing claims are plausible, but still under-documented.
The manuscript states that specimens are printed on a Bambu Lab H2D with PLA struts and TPU tendons in a single build, and that the support strategy uses a lowered support threshold plus manually generated narrowing pillars for near-vertical TPU members. That is useful. The Supplementary Information also helps by documenting the support-generation workflow and selected joint concept.

But Table 3 (print parameters) is still mostly TBD: nozzle temperatures, bed temperature, layer height, print speed, infill. For AM reviewers, these are not optional details. Without them, the repeatability and interface quality claims cannot be evaluated.

2. The internal-anchor joint is interesting, but at present it is a design hypothesis more than a validated contribution.
The core claim is that locating the TPU-PLA interface inside a rigid strut-end cage may improve durability relative to exposed overmold-like interfaces. That is mechanically plausible. The manuscript is also careful to say Ye et al. is only a precedent for co-printing, not validation of the present junction. Good.

However, the contribution statement still says this joint geometry is intended to improve cyclic interface durability, while multiple TODOs note that pull-out and fatigue verification remains outstanding. Until those tests are done, the paper should phrase the joint contribution as a proposed manufacturable junction architecture, not as a demonstrated durability advance.

3. The manuscript does a good job acknowledging resolution limits, but the consequences should be pushed further.
The as-built-resolution paragraph is one of the better revisions in this round. It admits that d_s and d_t are only nominally continuous and that realized diameters are quantized by nozzle/line-width/support interactions. Good. That honesty matters.

What is still needed is a clearer statement of how large the as-built spread is relative to the BO step size. If the dimensional noise is a substantial fraction of the search-space granularity, then the effective optimization problem is partly process optimization, not just geometry optimization.

4. FDM repeatability and interfacial reliability remain open gates.
The Results section has a placeholder for reproducibility across print replicates, which is exactly the right thing to report. Until those data exist, the paper cannot support strong claims about BO convergence on a noisy manufacturing platform. Likewise, interface strength characterization is still pending. Those are major gates.

5. The metal-analog plan is conceptually useful, but it does not validate the polymer printing process.
The Al/SS study may validate rank transfer of the geometry concept. It does not substitute for polymer-process validation. The manuscript mostly respects that distinction, but I would keep it explicit.

6. The newly integrated tensegrity-AM references are used reasonably well, but the manuscript should avoid flattening important differences among them.
Bauer, Pajunen 2021, and Sabouni-Zawadzka establish additive manufacturability and tunable/static behavior in tensegrity-inspired systems. Almeida and Davami are closer to dynamic loading relevance. Wang 2026 is closest in spirit to integrated rigid-flexible fabrication/validation. The manuscript mostly handles this correctly. I would just ask the authors not to compress these papers into a generic “therefore our process is established” statement. They establish the architecture class, not the specific PLA/TPU H2D process window or the internal-anchor joint.

Minor comments

1. The support strategy for near-vertical TPU tendons is important enough that one concise quantitative success criterion in the main paper would help.

2. If feasible, report actual measured tendon/strut diameters from a pilot batch, even before the full BO campaign, because that would ground the “realized geometry” discussion.

3. The statement that all specimens are printed vertically and arranged 3x3 on one plate is useful. Consider also reporting whether part location on the plate is randomized or balanced across BO batches to avoid spatial process bias.

4. The selected dovetail joint with anchor-bulb backup is described in the SI. That is fine, but one small schematic of the selected joint in the main text could help readers who never open the SI.

Bottom line

The AM framing is much better and the literature gap is closed. The paper is still gated by incomplete process disclosure and absent validation data on repeatability and interface durability. With those added, the paper could become solid. Without them, the manufacturing claims remain partly aspirational.
