\begin{center}
{\Large \textbf{Closed-Loop Bayesian Optimization of Multi-Material\\
3D-Printed Tensegrity-Inspired Energy Absorbers}}

\vspace{1em}
\textit{ASME IDETC-CIE 2026 --- Presentation-Only Abstract}

\vspace{0.5em}
\textit{Track: DAC-10 --- Design of Engineering Materials and Structures}
\end{center}

\vspace{1em}

Tensegrity-inspired architectures, in which rigid struts are suspended
within a continuous flexible network, can exhibit tunable nonlinear
mechanical responses and favorable strength-to-weight characteristics,
motivating their use in lightweight protective and energy-absorbing
structures.  Multi-material fused deposition modeling (FDM) can now
co-print rigid (PLA) struts and flexible (TPU) elements in a single
build, but the resulting design space---spanning strut geometry,
tension-element cross-section, connectivity topology, and unit-cell
tiling---is too large for efficient trial-and-error experimentation.

This work presents a closed-loop experimental campaign guided by
Bayesian optimization (BO) for developing multi-material 3D-printed
tensegrity-inspired structures optimized for energy absorption under
quasi-static compression and drop-weight impact loading.  The key
contribution is a design--print--test loop that operates directly on
physical measurements without relying on finite-element simulation: a
Gaussian-process surrogate model is trained on experimental measurements
of peak transmitted force, specific energy absorption (SEA), and
compaction efficiency, and an acquisition function recommends the next
batch of designs after each test round.  The workflow is largely
automated---design generation, surrogate updating, and
acquisition-function optimization proceed without operator input, while
specimen loading and fixture placement remain the primary manual steps.

We define a parameterized family of unit cells using a core-wrapping
strategy in which rigid PLA struts are enclosed by continuous TPU skins
to improve interfacial interlocking.  Design variables include strut
diameter and length, tension-element width and thickness, number of
struts per unit cell, and tiling pattern.  Specimens are fabricated on a
consumer-grade multi-material FDM system and tested under quasi-static
compression and instrumented drop-weight impact.

We will report the progression of BO-selected designs across iterations,
the evolution of the surrogate model as data accumulate, and
Pareto-efficient trade-offs between SEA and peak transmitted force
within the explored design space.  We will also discuss practical
lessons for operating a closed-loop physical experiment, including
print-failure handling, batch-to-batch variability, and
exploration--exploitation balance.
