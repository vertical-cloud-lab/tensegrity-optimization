\begin{center}
{\Large \textbf{Closed-Loop Bayesian Optimization of Multi-Material\\
3D-Printed Tensegrity-Inspired Energy Absorbers}}

\vspace{1em}
\textit{ASME IDETC-CIE 2026 --- Presentation-Only Abstract}

\vspace{0.5em}
\textit{Track: DAC-10 --- Design of Engineering Materials and Structures}
\end{center}

\vspace{1em}

Tensegrity-inspired architectures, in which rigid struts are suspended
within a continuous flexible network, can exhibit tunable nonlinear
responses and high strength-to-weight ratios, motivating their use in
lightweight energy-absorbing structures.  Multi-material fused
deposition modeling (FDM) can co-print rigid PLA struts and flexible TPU
elements in a single build, but the resulting design space---spanning
strut geometry, tension-element cross-section, topology, and unit-cell
tiling---is too large to explore by trial and error.

This work presents a closed-loop experimental campaign using Bayesian
optimization (BO) to develop multi-material 3D-printed
tensegrity-inspired structures for improved energy absorption under
quasi-static compression and drop-weight impact.  A Gaussian-process
surrogate trained on peak transmitted force, specific energy absorption
(SEA), and compaction efficiency recommends each next batch of designs,
enabling sequential design selection without finite-element simulation.
The workflow combines automated surrogate updating and experiment
selection with manual specimen handling and test setup.

We parameterize a family of unit cells in which rigid PLA struts are
enclosed by continuous TPU skins to improve interfacial interlocking.
Design variables include strut diameter, length, tension-element width
and thickness, strut count per cell, and tiling pattern.  Specimens are
fabricated on a multi-material FDM system and tested under quasi-static
compression and instrumented drop-weight impact.

We will report how BO-selected designs evolve across iterations, how
surrogate predictions change as data accumulate, and what
Pareto-efficient trade-offs emerge between SEA and peak force.  We will
also discuss practical lessons for the closed-loop workflow, including
print-failure handling, batch variability, and exploration--exploitation
balance.
