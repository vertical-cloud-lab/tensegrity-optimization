# BYU Outreach Plan: Dropping a Scaled-Up Tensegrity from the Kimball Tower

This note answers the two questions raised in the project issue:

1. **Who do we reach out to at BYU to get permission to drop a scaled-up tensegrity
   structure from the Kimball Tower?**
2. **What is the "CMR," and how would it help with a video?**

> ⚠️ **Verify before acting.** The contacts and phone numbers below are starting
> points compiled from public BYU webpages (links provided). Confirm the current
> owner of each process directly — BYU reorganizes offices periodically, and a
> high-visibility object drop from a 12-story building will be routed through
> formal risk-approval channels regardless of who you talk to first.

---

## 1. The building: Spencer W. Kimball Tower (KMBL / SWKT)

- The "Kimball Tower" is the **Spencer W. Kimball Tower**, building code **KMBL**
  (older signage/abbreviation **SWKT**). Completed 1981, ~161 ft, 12 floors — it
  is the **tallest building on BYU's Provo campus**.
  ([University Relations](https://ur.byu.edu/spencer-w-kimball-tower))
- It is an occupied academic/office tower (primarily College of Family, Home, and
  Social Sciences and College of Nursing), **not** an engineering building. Any
  drop has to account for pedestrians, windows, landscaping, and the building's
  current tenants/occupants.

Because this is a deliberate drop of an object from height in a public area, it
is treated as a **high-risk / special-use activity**. Expect to need sign-off
from *several* offices, not just one person.

**Practical sequencing:** raise it with **Hill** first → submit a formal request
to **Risk Management & Safety** (they will tell you exactly what they require) →
coordinate building/roof access with **Physical Facilities Building Operations**
→ loop in the **tenant colleges** and **University Communications** once a date
and safety plan exist.

### Likely conditions to plan for
- A written safety plan: cordoned exclusion/landing zone, spotters, tethered or
  recoverable specimen, weather/wind limits, and a go/no-go authority.
- Certificate of insurance / liability acknowledgment via Risk Management.
- Scheduling outside peak pedestrian hours; possibly a weekend.
- A named responsible faculty member present on site (Hill).

## 3. The "CMR" and getting a video

**CMR = the BYU [Compliant Mechanisms Research](https://compliantmechanisms.byu.edu/)
group** (Department of Mechanical Engineering, led by Prof. Larry Howell). It is
*not* a media/communications office — it is a mechanical-engineering research
group. It is relevant here because the CMR group is well known for producing
**high-quality video and photography of engineering demonstrations**, with
**students directly involved in filming, building props/prototypes, and producing
the media** (e.g., their [video gallery](https://compliantmechanisms.byu.edu/videos)
and [YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@byucmr), including collaborations
with creators like Mark Rober and Veritasium).

So "involving the CMR to get a video, with students assisting with filming" most
likely means: **leverage the CMR group's media experience and student videographers**
to capture and edit the drop test — a natural fit since both groups are in BYU
Mechanical Engineering and Prof. Hill can make a direct, internal introduction.

**How to engage them for the video:**
- Ask **Prof. Hill** to introduce the project to **Prof. Larry Howell / the CMR
  group** ([compliantmechanisms.byu.edu](https://compliantmechanisms.byu.edu/)).
- Scope the ask: high-speed and standard video of the drop, b-roll of fabrication,
  and student involvement in filming/editing.
- If official BYU promotion/press is desired in addition to CMR's own media,
  coordinate with **University Communications / University Relations** (Step 5
  above) — they handle official campus filming and outreach.

> If by "CMR" the team actually meant a campus media/communications office rather
> than the Compliant Mechanisms Research group, the correct counterpart is
> **University Communications / University Relations** ([ur.byu.edu](https://ur.byu.edu)).
> Confirm the intended meaning with the issue author.

## 4. One-paragraph summary

To drop a scaled-up tensegrity from the **Spencer W. Kimball Tower (KMBL/SWKT)**,
start with the faculty PI (**Jeff Hill**), then get formal approval from **BYU
Risk Management & Safety** (the gatekeeper for high-risk/special-use activities),
coordinate building and roof access with **Physical Facilities Building
Operations**, and inform the **KMBL tenant colleges** and **University
Communications**. For the video, **CMR** is the **Compliant Mechanisms Research**
group in Mechanical Engineering — known for student-produced engineering video —
and Prof. Hill can introduce the team to **Prof. Larry Howell's** group; involve
**University Communications** as well if official BYU promotion is wanted.
