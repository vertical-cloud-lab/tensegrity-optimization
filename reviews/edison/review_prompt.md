# Prompt submitted to Edison Scientific

Please provide a rigorous scientific review of the following conference abstract, intended for submission to a TMS 2027 (The Minerals, Metals & Materials Society) symposium. The work concerns tensegrity-like hexagonal lattice structures fabricated by multi-material FDM 3D printing (PLA struts + TPU tension elements), optimized via Bayesian optimization for impact/energy absorption, applied to Mars lander payload protection.

In your review address: (1) scientific soundness and novelty vs. the tensegrity, architected/cellular materials, energy absorption, and Bayesian-optimization literature; (2) clarity/precision of terminology (e.g., 'non-prismatic tensegrity', 'tensegrity-like lattice', 'high fidelity materials', 'anisotropic properties'); (3) whether claims are adequately supported and what quantitative metrics (specific energy absorption, peak transmitted force, compaction efficiency) should be reported; (4) gaps or missing citations; (5) concrete, actionable suggestions to strengthen the abstract. Conclude with a short list of the most relevant recent references.

ABSTRACT:
We present an exploration of non-prismatic tensegrity configurations in order to improve the impact absorption capabilities of tensegrity structures. We currently use a hexagon-based, tensegrity-like lattice arrangement and FDM fabrication to create a prototype TPU and PLA structure capable of protecting a payload under high-impact conditions. To improve payload protection for future Mars landers, our goal is to supplement future Mars exploration techniques through impact absorption in a Mars lander using Bayesian optimization of lattice base-unit geometry. The designs are validated using controlled drop testing devices, as well as real-world field demonstrations in which we use high fidelity materials for testing these structures. Structures are dropped from different angles to evaluate their anisotropic properties. The prototype is being optimized for consistent impact absorption regardless of landing orientation. Preliminary experimental results demonstrate successful payload protection across a range of testing conditions and design parameters.
