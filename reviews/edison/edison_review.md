### 1. Scientific Soundness and Novelty

The abstract brings together four research threads—tensegrity mechanics, multi-material FDM fabrication, Bayesian optimization of lattice geometry, and Mars lander payload protection—each of which has significant existing literature that the abstract does not acknowledge or position against.

**Tensegrity for planetary landing.** The concept of using tensegrity structures as impact-absorbing landing systems for planetary exploration is well established. The NASA SUPERball program demonstrated tensegrity-based robots specifically designed for planetary entry, descent, landing, and surface exploration more than a decade ago (zhang2021optimizationforenergy pages 1-2). Goyal et al. Garanger et al. Zhang et al. (2025) experimentally validated spherical tensegrity structures for cushioning performance in landing applications (zhang2025designandcushioning pages 1-2, zhang2025designandcushioning pages 19-21, zhang2025designandcushioning pages 21-22). Rimoli (2016) studied the impact tolerance of tensegrity-based planetary landers. The abstract does not cite any of this prior work and therefore overstates the novelty of the application domain.

**3D-printed tensegrity-inspired structures.** Pajunen et al. (2019) is a highly cited foundational study (100 citations) that designed, fabricated, and impact-tested 3D-printable "tensegrity-inspired" structures, demonstrating that monolithic printed structures can replicate key tensegrity characteristics including post-buckling stability and elastic recovery (pajunen2019designandimpact pages 2-3, pajunen2019designandimpact pages 8-9, pajunen2019designandimpact pages 1-2, pajunen2019designandimpact pages 3-4). That work carefully distinguished the printed structures from true pin-jointed, prestressed tensegrity systems. The present abstract does not make this distinction.

**Multi-material PLA/TPU FDM lattices.** The combination of rigid PLA with flexible TPU in multi-material FDM lattice structures for energy absorption has been explored by multiple groups. Abbas et al. (2026) systematically investigated PLA/TPU cubic lattice crashworthiness using standard metrics such as specific energy absorption (SEA), mean crush force (MCF), and crush force efficiency (CFE) (abbas2026multimaterialintegrationfor pages 3-5, abbas2026multimaterialintegrationfor pages 2-3, abbas2026multimaterialintegrationfor pages 19-20, abbas2026multimaterialintegrationfor pages 9-11). Yavas et al. (2022) demonstrated multi-material architected lattices with tunable stiffness, strength, and energy absorption. Khatri and Egan (2024) studied ABS/TPU multi-material honeycombs, including hexagonal geometries, quantifying energy absorption under both in-plane and out-of-plane loading. The abstract's use of PLA struts with TPU tension elements is therefore not unprecedented and requires clear differentiation from these prior works.

**Bayesian optimization of lattice structures.** Bayesian optimization for designing architected materials is an active field. Vangelatos et al. ### 3. Whether Claims Are Adequately Supported and Required Quantitative Metrics

The abstract's final sentence—"Preliminary experimental results demonstrate successful payload protection across a range of testing conditions and design parameters"—is entirely qualitative. For a TMS audience, the following quantitative metrics are expected for any energy-absorption or crashworthiness study:

- **Specific Energy Absorption (SEA)** in J/g or J/kg: This is the standard mass-normalized metric for comparing energy-absorbing structures. In the current literature, additively manufactured 3D lattices achieve SEA values up to approximately 38.9 J/g under dynamic loading, with TPU-based structures reaching the highest energy absorption efficiencies (bustihan2026recentadvancesin pages 19-21).
- **Peak Transmitted Force or Acceleration:** For a payload-protection application, the critical design output is the maximum force or g-load transmitted to the payload. This must be reported and compared to a survival threshold.
- **Crush Force Efficiency (CFE):** The ratio of mean crush force to peak crush force; values approaching 1.0 indicate ideal, constant-force energy absorption. Multi-material PLA/TPU lattices have been shown to achieve CFE values of 80–97% (abbas2026multimaterialintegrationfor pages 19-20).
- **Compaction Efficiency / Densification Strain:** How much stroke is available before densification.
- **Reusability / Elastic Recovery:** Whether the structure can survive multiple impacts, which is relevant for a lander that may bounce.
- **Orientation Dependence:** If the goal is orientation-independent payload protection, a quantitative metric (e.g., coefficient of variation of SEA or peak force across angles, or a quasi-isotropy index) must be defined and reported.
- **Impact Velocity and Energy:** The drop height, impact velocity, and total kinetic energy at impact must be specified and compared to Mars-relevant descent velocities.

The claim of "successful payload protection" is not scientifically meaningful without these metrics. Likewise, the Bayesian optimization component requires reporting of convergence behavior, number of evaluations, the objective function, design variable bounds, and comparison to a baseline design.
