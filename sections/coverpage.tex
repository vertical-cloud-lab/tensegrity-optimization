\subsection*{Abstract}

We propose a two-year mentored research program in which 2--3 undergraduate
students design and optimize multi-material 3D-printed tensegrity structures for
energy absorption.  Tensegrity structures---assemblies of rigid compression
members (PLA) and flexible tension members (TPU)---offer tunable mechanical
responses and high strength-to-weight ratios.  Using a multifidelity Bayesian
optimization framework, students will combine finite element simulations
(low-fidelity) with physical experiments (high-fidelity) to efficiently navigate
the high-dimensional design space.  Students will gain hands-on experience in
CAD design, multi-material 3D printing, impact testing, computational modeling,
and data-driven optimization.  Expected outcomes include undergraduate
conference presentations (UCUR, ASME IDETC), a peer-reviewed journal
submission, and preliminary data for a follow-on NSF proposal.  This project
provides a rich mentored learning environment at the intersection of structural
mechanics, advanced manufacturing, and machine learning.

% --- Relationship to External Funding ---
\subsection*{Relationship to External Funding}

This MRG will serve as seed funding for a future NSF proposal to the Civil,
Mechanical, and Manufacturing Innovation (CMMI) division, targeting the
Mechanics of Materials and Structures program.  The MRG-funded research will
generate preliminary experimental data, validated simulation models, and
proof-of-concept results for multifidelity optimization of architected
materials---key components needed for a competitive NSF proposal.  We anticipate
submitting the NSF proposal in Year~2 of this project.
